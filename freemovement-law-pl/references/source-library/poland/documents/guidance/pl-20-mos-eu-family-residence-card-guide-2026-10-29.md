## Scope and route distinction

This is an administrative procedure for a third-country national or stateless person applying for a residence card as a qualifying family member of a mobile EU/EEA/Swiss citizen in Poland. It is distinct from the ordinary national temporary/permanent/long-term EU residence-permit process submitted through the new MOS online portal. The card procedure remains an in-person application to the competent voivode. Do not route an EU-family card application through the electronic permit system merely because both use the MOS name.

The procedure confirms a residence right where the qualifying EU citizen satisfies the underlying residence conditions; the card documents that status. It does not itself create all substantive rights, and registration of address (meldunek), EU citizen residence registration, and the family member's card are separate procedures.

## Confirmed procedural outline

The current official MOS search result and the MSWiA page state, in summary:

1. **Competent authority and filing:** Apply in person to the voivode competent for the EU citizen's place of stay. The application is due no later than the day after the first three months from entry into Poland. The MOS guide says that an application cannot currently be submitted online. The voivode issues a certificate of application without delay.
2. **Personal appearance and biometrics:** The applicant normally signs and files in person, shows the original valid travel document, and provides fingerprints. A child aged six or older must attend. The authority may waive personal appearance in a special case, including documented health grounds. Appointment methods vary by voivodeship.
3. **Core materials:** Application form; valid travel document (original for inspection and copy); four current biometric photographs; and evidence that the EU citizen has registered residence or holds permanent-residence rights. Additional evidence must establish the applicable family relationship, dependency/care relationship where relevant, and the EU citizen's qualifying residence condition. The exact attachments depend on the applicant's relationship and case.
4. **Fee and decision time:** No fee is charged for issuing the card; a power-of-attorney stamp duty may apply. The card is issued within six months of filing according to the official guidance.
5. **Card validity and collection:** The card is valid for five years or the shorter intended stay of the EU citizen. Collection is generally personal and requires a valid travel document; special rules apply to minors and persons under guardianship. The card, together with a valid travel document, supports repeated visa-free border crossing into Poland, subject to applicable entry conditions.
6. **Noncompliance:** Official guidance states that failure to obtain or exchange a required card may be punishable by a fine.

This is a structured summary of official guidance. It is not a substitute for the current application form, statutory annex list or the competent voivode's appointment and document instructions.

## Eligibility and legal dependencies

The Polish EU-residence Act defines the relevant family-member categories. For an EU citizen, the core statutory categories include the spouse; qualifying direct descendants (including those of the spouse) under 21 or dependent; qualifying dependent direct ancestors (including those of the spouse); and the caregiver parent of a minor EU citizen who is dependent on that parent. The Act separately regulates Polish-citizen return cases; those require the statutory cross-border conditions and must not be treated as ordinary EU-citizen applications.

For EU citizens who are students, the family categories whose residence right derives under the Act are narrower than those for workers, self-employed or self-sufficient citizens. Verify the sponsor's status, family category and evidence against the current Act and Directive 2004/38/EC. EEA-EFTA and Swiss cases use related but distinct legal arrangements even where operational guidance groups them together.

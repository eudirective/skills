## EEA applicability boundary

Liechtenstein is an EEA EFTA State with a special movement-of-persons arrangement. The national law on implementation and promulgation of EEA rules (LR 170.51, `li-06`) says the rules applicable to Liechtenstein derive from the EEA Agreement and Joint Committee Decisions promulgated in the Liechtenstein State Law Gazette. It also provides for an electronic EWR Register. The external EWR Register reference (`li-04`) is not a retained register snapshot, and the separately identified EWR-RegV (`li-41`) is a reference note rather than the regulation text.

The local PFZG copy (`li-07`) says it implements Directive 2004/38/EC, the special EEA movement-of-persons solution, and a Liechtenstein–Switzerland framework. EFTA's EEA-Lex records Directive 2004/38/JCD 158/2007 as in force from 1 March 2009, with Liechtenstein's constitutional requirements recorded as fulfilled on 9 June 2008; the JCD itself says it leaves the Liechtenstein sectoral adaptations untouched. The local JCD 191/1999 copy (`li-05`) contains conditional-entry and transitional language ending in 2006. It must not be used alone to claim current quotas or residence conditions: reconcile it with later EEA decisions and the latest Liechtenstein Gazette/EWR Register before relying on those details.

The EFTA EEA-Lex screen checked 31 October 2026 lists Regulation (EU) 2025/1208 on identity cards/residence documents as under scrutiny, with a draft JCD under consideration by the EU and EFTA States. This remains an EEA incorporation dependency for Liechtenstein; do not treat the EU application date as Liechtenstein's EEA entry-into-force date.

The Visa Code is tracked as a Schengen dependency because VEV Article 1 applies subject to the Schengen acquis applicable to Liechtenstein. The Swiss movement-of-persons protocol and bilateral visa/representation instruments are separate again. Neither is an EEA incorporation shortcut.

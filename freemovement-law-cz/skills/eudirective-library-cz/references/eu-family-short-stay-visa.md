## 1. Scope of Czech EU-family visa procedure

This reference concerns an entry or short-stay visa to the Czech Republic, generally for a stay of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Keep separate: (a) whether Directive 2004/38/EC or a derived right applies; (b) whether the traveller needs a visa at all; (c) which Member State and Czech mission can examine the application; (d) whether the applicant is lodging or only seeking an appointment; and (e) any later Czech residence-card or residence-permit proceeding.

The central MFA page applies to visa applications lodged at Czech diplomatic missions. Its published procedure is not an exhaustive survey of mission-specific appointment, provider or checklist practices. A source-backed procedural difference is a review candidate, not by itself proof of illegality.

## 2. Family categories under Czech law

Act No. 326/1999 Coll. (`cz-esbirka-1999-326`), §15a(1), lists close-family relationships including a spouse; a parent caring for an EU citizen under 21; certain children under 21; and dependent direct descendants or ascendants. Section 15a(2) extends the national definition to listed close relatives of a Czech citizen permanently resident in Czechia. Section 15a(3) covers other relatives who meet dependency, household-membership or serious-health-care conditions, and partners with a duly attested durable relationship, including relevant relatives of a permanently resident Czech citizen.

The Czech statute's categories do not automatically equal Directive Article 2(2) or Article 3(2). Establish the citizen's movement and destination roles. A national extension for a Czech citizen who has not exercised free movement is not automatically an EU-derived right; returnee and other derived-rights situations require separate analysis. The English MFA page divides applicants into seven categories. Its categories 6 and 7 correspond to additional dependent/household/serious-health and durable-partner cases, but do not treat that administrative numbering as a complete EU-law classification.

Section 15b and the Withdrawal Agreement form a distinct UK-beneficiary route. Do not merge it into Directive 2004/38 without checking the applicable temporal and personal conditions.

## 3. National and Directive-linked visa exemptions

Before describing an application, check nationality-based Schengen visa exemption and any applicable national exemption. Directive Article 5(2) exempts a non-EU family member who holds a qualifying residence card issued under Article 10; the Commission's 2024 Visa Code Handbook, Part III, sections 2.1–2.2, treats a qualifying Article 20 permanent residence card as visa-exempt as well. Do not infer that an ordinary national residence permit is either card.

The Czech MFA's EU-family page also states that a family member may stay in Czechia visa-free for up to three months where they hold a qualifying Article 10 or Article 20 card and meet the stated stay condition, and in a special case where an expired Schengen visa holder remains in Czechia with the EU citizen for no more than three months. Verify the exact person, card, stay and statutory basis before relying on an exemption.

## 4. Competent consular authority

The Czech MFA's diplomatic-missions directory is the starting point for the responsible Czech mission. Competence can depend on the destination Member State, the applicant's country of lawful residence or presence, Czech representation arrangements, and the mission's consular district. Distinguish the Member State competent for the destination from the Czech mission territorially competent in the application country. Do not infer mission competence from nationality alone.

The accepted Czech pack contains a mission directory and two mission-specific examples, not an all-mission routing or current appointment matrix. Identify the applicant's country and responsible mission before stating current local filing details.

## 5. Lodging and appointment arrangements

The central MFA EU-family page directs applicants to Czech diplomatic missions but does not establish a uniform appointment, direct-lodging or provider arrangement for every mission. Check the responsible mission's current official instructions and any provider page the mission adopts. Identify whether the person has made an enquiry, requested an appointment, attempted to lodge, or obtained proof of formal lodging. Do not treat an appointment request or web form as the start of a statutory processing clock without the applicable rule.

The Commission Handbook, Part III, section 4.2, interprets EU-family access obligations to require a genuine and effective free direct route to the consulate; a qualifying applicant cannot be forced to pay for a premium appointment or paid external-provider appointment. Voluntary provider services may be charged, and the Member State remains responsible for its contracted provider's public-facing information. This Handbook is official Commission operational guidance, not binding legislation. Check the exact category, mission route and provider facts before applying it.

## 6. Application forms and supporting documents

The central Czech MFA page lists a Schengen application form, valid travel document, photograph, proof of the EU citizen's identity, evidence of the claimed family relationship, and evidence that the applicant will accompany or join the EU citizen. It says “or other” relationship evidence. It states that this procedure and list do not apply to its categories 6 and 7, which are directed to the family/friends Schengen route.

For a qualifying Directive beneficiary, the Commission Handbook, Part III, sections 4.6–4.7, distinguishes proof of beneficiary status from ordinary Visa Code supporting documents. It describes proof of identity, family relationship/category and accompanying or joining. It also states that applicants may choose documentary evidence for family ties and that other means of proof should not be refused merely because a specific document is unavailable. Translation may be requested if the authority does not understand the language. Legalisation or verification generally requires a specific authenticity concern; systematic requirements need an objectively supported, proportionate basis. The Handbook says ordinary journey-purpose, accommodation, means-of-subsistence and intention-to-leave documents are not required as routine support once the Directive route applies. Apply these points only after category and scope are established; do not erase the obligation to prove the relevant family category.

A mission checklist is evidence of that mission's published procedure, not automatically a complete statement of binding proof rules. Record each demanded item, its stated purpose and legal basis, category, source and any exception. A checklist that seems exclusive or imports ordinary tourist requirements is a potential restrictive-practice candidate for EU-core comparison, not a finding of illegality.

## 7. Fees and intermediary arrangements

The Czech MFA central EU-family page says lodging the visa application is free of charge. Keep that visa fee separate from optional provider services. Under the Commission Handbook, Part III, sections 4.1–4.2, a qualifying EU-family applicant cannot be charged the visa fee or compelled to pay for a provider appointment; optional services may be charged if the free direct route remains genuine and effective. Confirm any mission-specific provider arrangement and amount on the responsible official mission page. The accepted Czech pack does not establish a current all-mission fee/provider matrix.

## 8. Processing periods and extensions

The Czech MFA central page currently publishes 14 days from submission and says the period may be extended to 45 days in well-founded instances. Treat these as published official administrative guidance unless the current legal text establishes a particular statutory basis; the accepted source mapping does not certify that 14 days is a statutory deadline.

Directive Article 5(2) requires necessary visas to be issued free of charge as soon as possible through an accelerated procedure. The Commission Visa Code Handbook (C(2024) 4319 final, Part III, section 4.4) says processing beyond 15 days should be exceptional and duly justified and delays beyond four weeks are not reasonable. It is interpretive operational guidance and does not create an absolute statutory 15-day deadline. The published Czech 45-day extension therefore raises a potential compatibility question requiring a case-specific explanation; it is not automatically unlawful in every case. Record the actual lodging date, any extension ground, category, processing steps and explanation separately from pre-lodging appointment delay.

## 9. Refusal grounds

Act 326/1999, §20(5), lists grounds for refusing a short-stay visa application by a family member described in §15a(1) or (2), including specified document, health, security/public-order, abuse/fraud and relationship/accompanying-or-joining grounds. The central MFA page summarizes these grounds and says a decision is not automatic. Read the current text and identify the precise written ground; do not attribute a hypothetical ground to an authority.

Section 20(6) treats §15a(3) applicants separately: it refers to the ordinary directly applicable Visa Code requirements and states there is no legal entitlement to the short-stay visa. Compare that national route with the applicable Directive Article 3(2) facilitation and individual-examination duties; do not assume the national category itself decides the EU question.

## 10. Reconsideration and judicial review

Section 20(7) provides written notice of a 15-day period to ask the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for a new assessment under §180e, followed by the possibility of judicial review. The central MFA English EU-family page describes a court challenge within 30 days after rejection of the review request and refers to the regional court for the intended residence. Czech MFA mission pages may describe court venue differently. The accepted coverage model records no exhaustive remedy/deadline crosswalk. Verify the current statutory text, the exact decision, service date, applicant category and competent court immediately; do not generalize venue or deadline from one page. A complaint, SOLVIT contact or information request should not be assumed to stop a formal deadline.

## 11. Border applications and entry safeguards

Section 20(4) expressly refers to a family member under §15a(1) or (2) accompanying or joining the EU citizen as eligible to lodge a short-stay application at a border crossing. The text does not include §15a(3) in that paragraph. Check the precise category and current border rule before advising on border lodging.

Directive Article 5(4) requires a Member State, before turning a Union citizen or family member back for missing travel documents or visa, to give a reasonable opportunity to obtain documents or have them brought, or to prove coverage by other means. Apply this safeguard to the actual border facts and the relevant EU-law scope. The interaction between §20(4), extended-family facilitation and the border safeguard needs category-specific source review; do not characterize it as an established conflict based only on the difference in wording.

## 12. Extended-family procedures

For Czech-law §15a(3) applicants and MFA categories 6–7, identify the relevant dependency, household, serious-health or durable-partner condition and the evidence submitted. Article 3(2) beneficiaries have no automatic Directive right of entry equivalent to Article 2(2) family members, but the Member State must facilitate entry under national law and examine relevant personal circumstances. Compare the actual Czech criteria, ordinary-list instructions and individual handling with that obligation. The accepted source pack does not establish a complete provision-by-provision Article 3(2) implementation review; flag unresolved questions instead of assuming either full inclusion or exclusion.

## 13. Interaction with subsequent residence procedures

A Czech temporary residence permit/card for an EU citizen's family member is a separate procedure for stays over 90 days, generally administered by the Ministry of the Interior. The official MOI guidance distinguishes close and distant family categories, different residence documents and application requirements. It describes an in-country process, appointment and in-person filing steps, a published 60-day decision period subject to stoppages, and separate review rights. Do not import the visa's 14/45-day timing, MFA lodging route or short-stay checklist into the residence proceeding. Confirm if the user asks about entry, a visa, an Article 10/20 card, or a later Czech residence application.

## 15. Potential EU-law friction points

These are source-backed candidates for review, not pre-established violations:

1. **Processing:** the published 14-day period and possible 45-day extension should be compared with the binding accelerated-processing obligation and the Commission's non-binding 15-day/four-week guidance. The exact extension ground, justification and facts matter.
2. **Categories/checklist:** excluding Czech MFA categories 6 and 7 from the simplified list and directing them to the family/friends Visa Code route may require comparison with Article 3(2) facilitation and individual assessment. Their EU-law status and the actual applied requirements must be established first.
3. **Proof:** a mission checklist that treats one civil-status document, translation, legalisation, or a tourist document as exclusive or automatic may require review against the applicable category, freedom of proof and specific authenticity basis. No all-mission practice is established.
4. **Access/provider:** a mission or provider route that makes a paid intermediary or premium appointment unavoidable, or causes access delay inconsistent with effective accelerated processing, is a potential access concern. Identify the exact mission and free direct route before concluding anything.
5. **Border and remedies:** the limited wording of §20(4) and differences in published court-route descriptions merit precise category and source cross-checking. The source gap itself is not evidence of an unlawful outcome.

## Who the guidance treats as an EU citizen's family member

For this visa procedure, the MFA page includes:

1. a spouse;
2. a parent who actually cares for an EU citizen under 21;
3. a child under 21 of the EU citizen, or the EU citizen's spouse;
4. a dependent direct ascendant or descendant of the EU citizen or spouse, including a person dependent on essential care immediately before arrival in the Czech Republic;
5. a person related to a Czech citizen permanently registered in the Czech Republic in one of the relationships above;
6. a relative of an EU citizen, or of a Czech citizen permanently registered in the Czech Republic, who is dependent on that citizen in their country of origin, belongs to that citizen's household there, or urgently requires the citizen's personal care for serious health reasons; and
7. a person with a duly evidenced durable partnership with an EU citizen, or qualifying permanently resident Czech citizen, who accompanies or joins an EU citizen moving to or residing in another EU Member State.

This is the MFA page's visa-category description. It should be cross-checked against the governing Czech statute and the EU-law definition for the facts of a particular case.

## Visa-free short stays described by the page

The page says a qualifying family member may stay in the Czech Republic for up to 90 days without a visa when they hold an Article 10 family-member residence card or Article 20 permanent residence card issued by another EU Member State and the Czech stay does not exceed three months. It also describes a visa-free stay of up to three months where the family member is accompanying the EU citizen in the Czech Republic after their Schengen visa has expired.

## Application documents and processing

For the standard EU-family Schengen application, the page lists:

- the completed Schengen visa application form;
- a valid travel document;
- a photograph;
- evidence that the EU citizen exists, such as a copy of their identity card or passport;
- evidence of the family relationship or qualifying dependency, serious health need, or durable partnership; and
- evidence that the applicant will accompany or join the EU citizen in the Czech Republic.

The page gives a 14-day processing period from filing, extendable to no more than 45 days in justified cases, and says the application is free of charge. Applicants in the page's categories 6 and 7 follow the family-and-friends visitor-visa evidence route instead of the standard list above.

These are central instructions. The page does not set mission-specific appointment, filing-channel, translation, or local document rules.

## Examination and refusal grounds stated by the page

The MFA says applications are not decided automatically and that Czech diplomatic missions may investigate whether a statutory ground in section 20(5) of Act No. 326/1999 Coll. applies. The page lists these grounds:

- a forged or altered travel document;
- reasonable suspicion of a serious disease;
- a substantiated security risk or serious public-order risk;
- a relevant Schengen information-system alert followed by additional information supporting an ongoing security or serious public-order risk;
- circumvention of the Foreign Nationals Residence Act to obtain a visa, including a sham marriage or purpose-driven paternity acknowledgment;
- fraudulent conduct to obtain the visa, such as forged evidence or false interview statements; or
- discovery during examination that the applicant is no longer a family member or will not accompany or join the EU citizen.

The page discusses four forms of marriage-related abuse by reference to Commission Communication COM(2014) 604 and its handbook: a marriage entered into by both parties to misuse EU law; deception of the EU citizen about the intended family life; forced marriage, often linked to trafficking; and a purported marriage that is invalid and may involve forged or misused documents. It also points to section 341 of the Czech Criminal Code concerning assistance with unauthorised residence.

The list above records what the administrative page says. It does not resolve the interpretation, evidential threshold, proportionality, or application of any ground in an individual case.

## Reconsideration and court review

The page states that an EU-family applicant whose Schengen visa is refused may request reconsideration. If that request is rejected, the family member may bring an administrative action before the regional court within 30 days after receiving the reconsideration outcome. The court is identified by reference to the district where the applicant would perform the arrival-reporting duty after entry. The intended Czech residence address must be apparent from the visa application.

The general MFA refusal page supplies additional filing details and dates, but its general 15-day deadline must not be silently substituted for the special EU-family route described here. Check the current statute and the applicable refusal notice before calculating a deadline.

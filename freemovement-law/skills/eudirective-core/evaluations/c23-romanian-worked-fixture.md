# C23 Romanian/UAE review fixture — not a normal-use rule

### Worked pattern: Polish spouse / Romania / application in UAE

User account: Pakistani spouse of a Polish citizen intending to travel from Poland to Romania; the applicant will join there; consulate asked for bank statements, pre-booked flights, hotel reservations and travel medical insurance. Take those travel/family allegations as working facts. Assuming they hold, **a list for ordinary UAE Schengen applicants is inapplicable** to this EU-family visa, even if those items appear in such a list. Under Handbook III 4.7.3(5)-(6), finances, accommodation, and travel medical insurance are not compulsory EU-family supporting documents; under III 4.7.2 an advance paid ticket cannot be demanded to prove the EU citizen's future trip. A confirmation of intended movement can be appropriate. This is a rule-scope conclusion; actual instructions given at the counter remain evidence for a complaint, not a precondition to the rule-scope conclusion.

A separately expired UAE residence visa requires a **specific** question about lawful presence/residence on the application date and any grace period or pending regularisation; Handbook III 3.1 and Visa Code Article 6 may affect which consulate must accept the application. Do not equate expiry with proven unlawful presence. Regardless, that territorial-competence question does not make the bank/hotel/insurance demands permissible *as ordinary EU-family visa-support requirements*.

Romania's current national-specific published instructions, if used, must be checked at the official eVisa page (`https://eviza.mae.ro/info/SpecificRules`) and relevant current Romanian legislation; **do not encode Romanian rules as reusable EU/core rules**. In the worked example, the published rules say appointments are not imperative for EU-family applicants and list a limited supporting-document set. If the applicant attended in public opening hours and was turned away solely for lack of an appointment, identify that as inconsistent with Romania's published access procedure, subject to any factually relevant operational circumstances; request a written explanation rather than asserting that no one could lawfully be turned away for any reason.

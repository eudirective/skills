# C23 human-review reference — Romania / Dubai scenario

**Illustration only; the scenario is a retrospective, user-described case. This is not a new country-rule record, a legal adjudication or a mandatory verbatim answer.** Compare future model responses against the *logic*, not the prose.

## Legal position from the account

On the described facts, a Pakistani spouse travelling to join a Polish EU citizen going to Romania qualifies for the mobile-EU-family visa framework, assuming the marriage and intended joining are as stated. The spouses need not arrive together. **The ordinary UAE harmonised Schengen checklist does not apply to that EU-family visa.** Bank statements, hotel reservations, travel medical insurance and advance paid flight reservations cannot be imposed as routine EU-family visa-issuance evidence; the authority may ask for evidence of identity, family relationship and intended accompanying/joining, and a simple confirmation of the EU citizen's future travel can suffice when that travel is prospective. The official 2024 Visa Code Handbook Part III sections 4.7.2, 4.7.3(5)-(6), and Romania's current family-visa page support this scope distinction. Which exact ordinary list the officer referred to does not change the exclusion.

**Separately**, the expired UAE residence visa creates a *consular territorial competence* question under Visa Code Article 6, not an entitlement to demand tourist bank/hotel/insurance papers. Expiry by itself does not answer whether the applicant remained legally resident or lawfully present in the UAE. The authority must address that specific procedural question rather than leave the applicant without a comprehensible answer.

Romania's official eVisa EU-family page says an appointment is not imperative, and direct lodging during consular public hours is possible. If, as described, the applicant attended within those hours and was denied *solely* for lacking an appointment, that conflicts with the published arrangement. We should ask the mission to explain its rule and state whether it received the application, rather than rely on the online portal status alone.

## Immediate actions

Write briefly to the mission requesting (a) direct lodging or a prompt appointment without an ordinary-tourist checklist, (b) whether the application was received and entered as an application, (c) a written legal basis and the exact purpose of each contested document request, and (d) a written territorial-competence position if that is their reason. Preserve sent emails and dates. If describing removal, use first-hand and specific wording, ask for a neutral investigation and prompt preservation of possible CCTV and incident records, and distinguish this conduct complaint to the head of mission/foreign ministry from the free-movement complaint. SOLVIT may address the cross-border EU-rights aspect but is not an appeal and does not suspend deadlines. If the authority issued a formal written refusal, immediately check the national challenge route and service date.

## Questions that change the advice

1. Were you legally resident or otherwise lawfully present in the UAE on the two dates, including under a grace period or a pending regularisation decision?
2. Did the consulate actually **accept** the application on 9 October, issue a reference/receipt or written refusal, or decline to lodge it?
3. On 30 September, did you attend during public consular hours, and was the lack of an appointment the stated reason for denying access?

These questions do not postpone the already decidable legal conclusion concerning the ordinary checklist. Lack of CCTV or witnesses is not a reason to disregard the applicant's description; it affects how to pursue and document the conduct grievance.

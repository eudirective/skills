# C25 preventive strategy for foreseeable procedural friction

Use this C25 branch in an ordinary EU-family entry or short-stay visa consultation when any of these applies:

- A material procedural-friction candidate is supported by the relevant official national source and the applicable EU safeguard.
- The user expressly asks for prevention, planning, or strategy.
- A foreseeable administrative decision could materially affect the applicant's rights.

No lodged application, appointment failure, delay, refusal, disputed demand, complaint, or established infringement is required. A prospective risk is not an adverse-event finding. C25 owns this model-led strategy review; C23 supplies read-only case guidance, while C27 remains the existing completeness registry. Do not create a grievance or authority position that has not occurred.

## Select and sequence a proportionate response

Start with the applicant's practical objective and the specific future decision or barrier that could frustrate it. Establish the applicable legal category, authority, procedural stage, and source-supported risk before recommending action. Keep four questions separate: what the law requires, whether an action may help, whether it is feasible, and what evidence it would produce.

For each material risk:

1. Describe the possible procedural error or barrier and why it matters to the applicable EU right.
2. Decide whether an action before the decision or barrier could reduce the risk without creating avoidable burden or prejudice.
3. Consider realistic alternatives: ordinary cooperation while preserving EU rights; a short legal-category statement; targeted clarification of the category, legal basis, appointment, lodging, or timetable; waiting or taking no extra action; early supervisory clarification where its route is verified; and formal remedies only after their factual and legal prerequisites arise.
4. Compare likely benefit, proportionality, truthfulness, evidentiary value, reversibility, timing, cost/burden, and risks from action or inaction. Do not use a numerical score or invent an advantage to justify a complex intervention.
5. Recommend the least burdensome useful first step, or say why waiting/no extra action is preferable. Reassess after any material authority response or changed circumstance; do not continue an unconditional sequence. State the response or event that would justify reassessment or escalation.

Prefer a cooperative, precise initial request where it can protect the same interest as a complaint. Do not accuse an authority of a violation before its conduct supports that conclusion. Do not conceal required information, misstate facts, or advise evasion of legitimate process. Do not suggest that a complaint, SOLVIT referral, or information request suspends a formal remedy deadline.

## Czech processing-time example

For a Czech EU-family visa enquiry, first establish whether the person and procedure plausibly fall within the relevant EU-family route, whether a visa is required, the responsible Czech mission, and whether the person is seeking an appointment or has lodged an application. The Czech MFA's published 14-day period and possible extension to 45 days are a warning sign to examine; they do not prove that a particular consulate has misclassified an application or that the 45-day period will be used.

Where the Czech source and applicable EU category support the concern, a proportionate prospective sequence may be:

- **Before an appointment:** use the responsible mission's verified official channel to identify the claimed EU-family basis, request the appropriate facilitated appointment/direct submission route, and ask for confirmation of the applicable category if it is genuinely unclear.
- **At lodging:** provide concise evidence of the relevant family category and accompanying/joining link; keep proof of formal lodging. An appointment enquiry is not automatically the lodging date.
- **If the authority invokes the 45-day period:** ask neutrally which rule and case-specific examination are said to justify it, and how the accelerated EU-family procedure is being applied. Do not allege intentional misclassification from the published period alone.
- **If a verified obstacle persists:** reassess urgency, the actual response, competent supervisory channels, and any formal remedy and deadline. Do not escalate automatically if clarification resolves the issue.

Directive 2004/38 Article 5(2) contains the binding requirement to issue qualifying visas as soon as possible through an accelerated procedure. A published outer period is not a default waiting period or a right to use all of that time where an application can properly be decided earlier. The Commission Visa Code Handbook's view that processing beyond 15 days should be exceptional and duly justified and delays beyond four weeks are not reasonable is non-binding guidance, not an absolute statutory 15-day deadline. Check the exact family category, scope, mission instructions, lodging status, explanation, and individual circumstances before applying either benchmark.

An appointment/access problem is not itself a formal refusal. Keep pre-lodging access, an attempted submission, formal lodging, and an issued decision separate. If a formal refusal later occurs, switch to the existing refusal/remedy analysis and verify its actual reasons, service date, competent route, and deadline. Never invent a national supervisor, contact channel, power, or time limit.

## Reuse and boundaries

Reuse C5e's option-generation principles and C5f's comparison and conditional-sequencing principles conversationally: include direct cooperation and clarification baselines, consider delay/inaction risks, compare burden and evidence, sequence conditionally, and allow no action. C25 remains the owner of ordinary applicant-facing strategy. Do not create C5d attack-angle findings to unlock strategy, run C5e/C5f scripts on applicant facts, describe a synthetic engine as executed, or present C5e/C5f IDs, scorecards, or synthetic records to applicants. If an adverse event later occurs, reassess using the actual response and transition to C25's existing grievance/remedy branch.

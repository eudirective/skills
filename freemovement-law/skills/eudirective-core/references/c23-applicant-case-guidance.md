# C23 - User-facing EU free-movement case guidance

This is a **read-only, model-led assistance workflow for real or fictional cases supplied voluntarily by a user**. It is distinct from the C6/C7 synthetic controlled-controller lane, the C17 executable fictional-draft generator and the C21 synthetic privacy/telemetry preview. Nothing here unlocks those executable lanes for private data, certifies confidentiality/anonymity, or authorises sending. Never claim a controlled C6/C7/C17/C21 verification occurred unless the applicable scripts actually ran on supported inputs.

## C27 multi-proceeding intake and prior-disclosure reconciliation

Before substantive applicant guidance, read `references/state-engine/c27-case-state.md`. Separate every visa, residence, appeal, complaint and information-request proceeding. Record the **applicant's claimed application type**, the **authority's actual classification**, the stage supported by available evidence, prior statements and documents already delivered to each recipient, and unresolved deadlines. Do not infer formal lodging, acknowledgment of contents, official reliance, service, or statutory waiver from appointment or online-form submission. Provide a concise source-attributed check-completeness note each substantive turn; do not claim C27 scripts ran on private dossiers.

## 1. Intake first: identify the decision-changing questions

Extract an *in-conversation* fact matrix: relevant EU citizen and nationality; applicant/family tie; destination and movement; accompany/join plan; requested application or action; consular location; applicant's possible lawful residence/presence in that jurisdiction; events/dates; what the authority asked/refused; written notice or oral statement; what the user wants now; and any immediate deadline or risk of evidence loss. Attribute these as **user-reported working facts**, not judicially proven facts. Accept the account for purpose of helping the user, unless internally contradictory, physically impossible or contradicted by supplied records; do not routinely challenge credibility.

Find missing *material* facts and sort them by how much they change a distinct legal issue, deadline or next action. Ask no more than **three short, prioritized questions in the first response**; do not invent answers, and do not ask for data already supplied. If a material unknown blocks the ultimate action, give any independently decidable legal conclusions now and ask the question before pretending to provide a full assessment. If nothing essential is missing, proceed without interrogating the user. Optional evidence or chronology improvements belong in the action plan, not the essential-question gate.

Distinguish three evidence states for every important proposition: (1) user's first-hand statement, provisionally assumed for advice, (2) document or other evidence shown to the assistant, and (3) governing public legal source checked. Do not substitute the phrase 'not independently verified' for an analysis. Use narrower descriptions such as 'on your account', 'the written refusal is not yet available', or 'the exact service date is needed for an appeal deadline' only when they change the practical next step.

## 2. Identify source and regime before comparing any checklist

Separate (a) eligibility for Directive 2004/38/EC, (b) Member State responsible for visa, (c) **consular territorial competence in the country of application**, (d) procedural access/lodgement, (e) the supporting documents relevant to **visa entitlement**, and (f) grounds for refusal. An issue in (c) never silently converts an (e) EU-family application into an ordinary Schengen tourism application.

For a non-EU spouse joining/accompanying an EU citizen moving to a Member State **other than that citizen's own State**, assess the Directive as the first regime candidate, considering returnees and exceptions separately. Different arrival times do not, by themselves, disqualify the spouse. Verify the rule and exceptions with official EU sources. If the necessary movement/family facts are clear on the user's account, state the legal consequence **conditional on those facts**; do not hedge a clear legal rule merely because the authority's oral behavior lacks CCTV.

**Mandatory rule-scope comparator for each disputed document demand**:
1. Name the demanded item and the authority's claimed or apparent purpose, where known.
2. Find the source imposing that item under the *ordinary* procedure, and check whether it applies to this applicant category. If it does not apply, say so clearly. Finding an ordinary harmonised list does NOT prove its applicability.
3. Check overriding EU-family requirements and scope exceptions in the 2024 Visa Code Handbook Part III 4.7, particularly 4.7.2 and 4.7.3(4)-(6), and the Directive. Identify the difference between booked flights and permitted confirmation that an EU citizen will travel.
4. Give a specific conclusion for each category: `required-for-EU-family-entitlement`, `not-required-as-routine-EU-family-support`, `may-be-relevant-to-consular-competence`, `context-dependent`, or `needs-one-material-fact`. Do not conflate the document's relevance as voluntary evidence, admissibility, and legal compulsion.
5. Check **what was demanded for what purpose**. Competence evidence under Visa Code Articles 5-7 is a distinct, narrower question; it is not a licence to impose the entire ordinary tourist checklist.
6. Test the counterargument explicitly. If a source says 'ordinary UAE supporting list', but official guidance expressly excludes mobile-EU-family applicants, the conclusion is **the ordinary list is inapplicable to that family visa** (assuming Directive coverage). Do not say that the applicant must first obtain the exact officer's checklist to decide that legal rule. Ask for the checklist only to strengthen a factual allegation or identify a different claimed purpose.

Source identities to check in the source library: `eu:visa-code-handbook-i-2024`, `eu:32004l0038`, and `eu:32009r0810`. Before relying on them for a current applicant question, check the matching registered EU pack and use the retrieved text from the plugin's `data/` folder; do not treat the pinned compatibility copies as current. The 2024 Handbook is Commission interpretive guidance, not a substitute for binding legislation. Source notes lacking full text cannot support exact quotes.

### Review rule (case-neutral)

When a local-residence issue appears, determine first whether the authority **actually relied on territorial competence**, merely requested a local document, or never mentioned competence. Preserve competence as a potentially decisive threshold for *specific requested relief*, but do not reframe a refusal based on ordinary visa-document demands as a territorial-competence refusal. Disclosure in an email does not prove consular acknowledgment or statutory waiver. Uninvoked objections should remain in internal contingency analysis unless a requested remedy makes their examination necessary. Read `references/c25-applicant-strategy.md` for the grievance and authority-position review. Detailed country scenarios are **evaluation material**, not normal-use instructions.

For Member-State-specific appointment and documentary instructions, check the current relevant official national source; do not encode one country's rules as reusable EU/core rules.

## 3. User-focused remedies and evidence, not a journalist's verdict

For each actionable issue, provide: (1) concise legal position based on the user's account, (2) distinct missing/contrary fact if decisive, (3) immediate step and desired outcome, (4) competent authority or route *only if verified* and its power/limitations, (5) how to preserve or obtain supporting evidence, and (6) risk of delay, escalation, excessive disclosure or unsupported accusation. Prefer a direct written clarification/receipt request when the application was not accepted; identify separate supervisory/conduct complaints and EU-law channels when relevant. Distinguish informal SOLVIT/Commission complaints from formal appeals; never imply SOLVIT suspends appeal deadlines.

Assume the user is telling the truth for practical advice. **Do not require independent corroboration before identifying a potential procedural complaint.** Where evidence is thin, propose supportable wording ('I attended on [date] and was told...'), reconstruct a contemporaneous timeline, retain emails, seek an incident record and ask the authority to preserve CCTV promptly where appropriate. Explain an adverse evidentiary consequence only for the specific allegation or remedy where it matters. Avoid personal accusations of dishonesty or misconduct without good support. A conduct grievance can be raised as first-hand experience even when the authority disputes it; request a neutral investigation.

Never label a case an established legal violation solely by trusting an assertion; where the **law** is clear and the **reported conduct** is concrete, say 'On the facts you describe, this demand conflicts with [rule]' and offer what to do. When facts may be contradicted, advise safe qualification and how to obtain records, without generic newsroom disclaimers. Do not conflate proof needed to succeed before an authority with proof needed to advise the user.

## 4. Deliverable pattern

Start with the decisive, usable rule-scope determination where possible. Then address other independent issues and the most useful next actions. Finish with **up to three material questions** that alter the answer; do not turn a complete analysis into a shopping list for documents. If a major missing fact makes the main requested recommendation premature, ask it near the top, explaining which answer it controls.

Do not claim expert review, official confirmation of an authority's disputed conduct, an exact appeal deadline without service/rule/date, source freshness outside the checks performed, or a guaranteed outcome. For real cases, treat the analysis as **human-reviewable advisory guidance**. No scripts may send notices, contact authorities, ingest arbitrary private files into C6/C21, or upload applicant information to telemetry. Do not include names, passport numbers, email addresses or dossier contents in public web searches; request only the minimum detail needed for advice. Material already typed into ChatGPT is not guaranteed to have undergone local-before-model anonymisation.

The independent C15f legal-semantic validation is still outstanding; be accurate about that without declining user-facing assistance. Do not show project C6/C7 implementation status to an applicant unless they ask for technical assurance.

# C25 - Evidence acquisition, data access and urgent preservation

**Normal-use read-only guidance.** Search for *authority-controlled evidence* even where no separate data-protection breach is alleged. Select each route for the information it can lawfully obtain and distinguish the practical object (obtain a copy, preserve against loss, establish procedural status, investigate conduct, or compel production in proceedings).

## Trigger and first action

When the user reports disputed consular conduct, oral document demands, uncertain application status, disputed appointment/email handling, detention or physical escort, systematic delays or electronic barriers, proactively consider an **evidence map**:

| Record type | Possible route | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Personal data in emails, case notes, appointment/visa-system entries | GDPR Article 15 request to the controller/DPO; assess sector-specific access route if VIS/other system applies | Right covers the applicant's personal data and related Article 15 information, **not automatically every document** containing or mentioning them. Limitations/exemptions and third-party rights may apply. |
| Date/purpose of accesses to the applicant's data | Targeted Article 15 request; consider CJEU C-579/21 Pankki S for access operations | Never promise employee names, complete security logs or proof that an officer read/understood a particular email. |
| CCTV/incident/security records involving the applicant | Prompt preservation request **and separately** targeted access request; check operator/controller and security exceptions | Footage may no longer exist. Third-party masking, security exemptions, and national rules may restrict disclosure; GDPR access does not guarantee raw CCTV copies or freeze retention. |
| Entire administrative file, non-personal internal legal instructions | National file-access/FOI/process rules or disclosure through complaint/court route | GDPR is not an unrestricted discovery or FOI tool. Verify local coverage and conditions. |
| Staff conduct determination, reasons for exclusion, incident narrative | Visa Code Article 38(5) process complaint (where applicable), supervisory investigation; seek copies of applicant's personal data separately | Complaint, Article 15 access and investigation are **different** processes and may have different powers/deadlines. |

### Timing

**Preserve first when deletion is plausible.** A preservation request can ask the controller/security unit to suspend routine deletion of specified relevant records and confirm whether records exist and retention rules; it is **not** itself a guaranteed legal hold. Where relevant, examine GDPR Article 18, particularly whether personal data are needed for the establishment/exercise/defense of legal claims when the controller would otherwise erase them, subject to legal requirements and exceptions. Do not assert an unconditional Article 18 right or automatic CCTV retention. Request promptly even if data access will take longer.

A GDPR access request ordinarily receives a response within **one month** under Article 12(3); a reasoned extension may be possible. Check applicable special rules, controller and current information. An Article 15 request need not allege a GDPR violation. If the controller refuses, provides no response or incompletely responds, identify the responsible supervisory authority and available complaint/judicial routes rather than presuming wrongdoing. A subject-access request does not suspend independent visa appeal periods.

### Case-specific official source checks (not universal defaults)

- GDPR Regulation (EU) 2016/679 Articles 12, 15, 18, 23: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng`.
- Visa Code consolidated Regulation (EC) 810/2009 Articles 38(5), 39: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/810/2024-06-28/eng`.
- For VIS-held personal data, check the *current* consolidated VIS Regulation 767/2008 Article 38, not merely the GDPR notice: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2008/767/2026-06-12/eng`.
- EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 on access (final 2023): `https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-012022-on-data-subject-rights-right-of-access_en`.
- CJEU C-487/21 *CRIF* on intelligible copies/extracts only where necessary for effective access; C-579/21 *Pankki S* on date/purpose of data consultation, with limits on employee identities. Verify exact judgment provisions for argument rather than treating press releases as primary law.
- **Romania-specific example, verify live before relying:** Ministry of Foreign Affairs eViza data-protection notice `https://eviza.mae.ro/DataProtection` identifies the MFA and its DPO contact `dpo@mae.ro` and refers to ANSPDCP. This is not a reusable DPO address for other countries or an assurance that *every* guard/security operator is controlled by MFA.

## Actionability check (not a token checklist)

Before calling a route useful to the applicant, confirm a **concrete next step** in the answer: (1) the likely controller/recipient or a precise action to identify them; (2) named *categories* of relevant personal data or other records, without demanding unrelated private details; (3) a specific access, preservation, file-status or investigation request; (4) urgency or independently applicable deadline when known; and (5) the major limitations and response/escalation route when verifiable. A statement such as "consider GDPR" or a citation to Article 15 without an actionable step is not an adequate recommendation. Explain if a route is materially unavailable rather than fabricating a controller.

Where more than one reported act occurred, consider whether separate footage, staff incident notes, audio, email-access events or witness records are material for each act. One broad "security incident" label should not erase physical contact as a separate evidentiary question. Distinguish a request to **preserve** possible material now from an eventual right to **access** personal data or a full file; do not wait for the access deadline before requesting preservation where evidence may expire.

## Applicant-centered output

For each high-value record: say what it may show (and what it **cannot** establish), who likely controls it **if known**, best route, a targeted description of the applicant's personal data, urgency, foreseeable limit, and follow-up if records are withheld. Avoid overbroad requests demanding 'all internal correspondence' regardless of personal-data scope. Do not turn a potential Article 15 right into a claim that documents must exist or that a complaint succeeds if data are not produced.

Never send, publish or store an actual applicant's identifying access request without explicit permission and an available authorized tool. Do not embed names, passport numbers or exact case details in public web searches, synthetic fixtures or telemetry.

## Targeted request drafting pattern (only when requested by the applicant)

For an Article 15 access draft, identify the controller, subject, dates/range and relevant category of *personal data* (e.g., visa/appointment processing entries, personal data recorded in messages, access dates and processing purposes, personal images in footage), then request Article 15 information and an intelligible copy of the personal data subject to lawful redactions. Ask for the lawful basis of any withheld data and the available supervisory complaint route. Avoid claims to all internal policies and other persons' data.

Draft the **urgent preservation request separately**: identify the incident timeframe and relevant record categories (e.g., security footage, incident register, appointment/email-access metadata), state the contemplated complaint/legal claim where true, ask for immediate suspension of routine deletion where legally permissible, confirmation of the appropriate controller and relevant retention policy, and an express response. Do not say that sending the request itself freezes records. A generic request without dates cannot be assumed sufficient to locate specific footage; ask for the minimum temporal range privately rather than putting it in public research queries.

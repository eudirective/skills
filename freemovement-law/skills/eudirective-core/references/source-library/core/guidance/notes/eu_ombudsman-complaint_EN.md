## Can the Ombudsman help me? If not, who else could?
If the Ombudsman is not able to help with the issue you are facing, there are other bodies that may be able to assist.

## Make a complaint to the European Ombudsman
The European Ombudsman investigates complaints from individuals, businesses and organisations about maladministration by the institutions, bodies and agencies of the European Union .
A complaint must be made within two years of the date when the person affected became aware of the facts.
Prior approaches: The complainant must first have contacted and tried to resolve the matter with the institution in question.
To ensure optimum service, only complaints submitted using the online complaint form below will be processed. Please note that complaints sent by email will not be accepted.

## What is maladministration?
Maladministration occurs if an institution or body fails to act in accordance with the law or the principles of good administration, or violates human rights.
Maladministration can include administrative irregularities, unfairness, discrimination or the abuse of power, for example in the managing of EU funds, procurement or recruitment policies. It also includes the failure to reply, or the refusal or unnecessary delay in granting access to information in the public interest.

## What the European Ombudsman cannot do
The European Ombudsman cannot investigate complaints against national, regional or local administrations in the Member States, even when the complaints are about EU matters.
The Ombudsman cannot investigate matters that are subject to legal proceedings .

## Submit a complaint
For reasons of efficiency, the European Ombudsman operates an online complaints system.
Online complaints can be submitted in three easy steps:
1 Check the criteria
Go through our checklist for making a complaint and make sure that the European Ombudsman can investigate your complaint
2 Prepare the necessary information and documents in advance
In order to submit a complaint, you will need to provide the following information:
- The name of the EU institution about which you wish to complain.
- The decision or matter at issue in your complaint.
- What you consider that the EU institution or body has done wrong.
- What, in your view, the institution should do to put things right.
- Whether the matter has already been settled by or is pending before a court.
For us to accept the complaint as complete, you will need to provide the following documentation :
- Evidence proving that you already contacted the EU institution or body concerned in order to obtain redress and you have given it a reasonable amount of time to reply. This is a mandatory condition for a complaint to be admissible. Until we receive this evidence, we will not be able to deal with your complaint.
- If appropriate, supporting documents about the decision or matter at issue in your complaint, and about when you became aware of it.
3 Create an account or login to an existing account
You need to create an account to submit complaints. Once you have an account, you can compile a complaint in your own time before submitting it. You can check the status of your complaint or submit new complaints from the same account.

## Submit a complaint (part 2)

### Guidelines for complaints
- Please keep your complaint concise. In exceptional cases, where a complaint needs to be longer than the allowed maximum number of words in the form, please include the full complaint as an attachment, and write a summary in the complaint form fields.
- If there are a number of issues that have gone wrong, please identify and number the various issues. Please provide arguments and evidence supporting each alleged wrongdoing. Please indicate clearly what steps you consider should be taken to resolve each matter complained about.
- Please provide only documents or information to the Ombudsman that are necessary for understanding the complaint.
- Please do not provide the Ombudsman with personal data of third parties unless the personal data is necessary to enable the Ombudsman to process the complaint.
Our dedicated online form enables us to deal with complaints more efficiently and faster. If you cannot use the online form, the European Ombudsman accepts complaints submitted by post.

### Submit your complaint by post
Please note that there are some complaints we cannot deal with. Therefore, even if you wish to submit a complaint by post, we advise you to go through the checklist for making a complaint section before sending your complaint.
If you cannot submit a complaint online, and wish to submit a complaint by post, please:
- Fill out the complaint form
- Provide only documents or information to the Ombudsman that are necessary for the understanding of the complaint.
- Please keep your complaint concise. In exceptional cases, where a complaint exceeds five pages, please include a short summary in the complaint form and number the pages of the attached complaint.
- Please do include:
- Evidence proving that you already contacted the EU institution or body concerned in order to obtain redress. This is a mandatory condition for a complaint to be admissible. You will be otherwise informed that we cannot deal with your complaint.
- If appropriate, supporting documents on the decision or matter at issue in your complaint, and about when you became aware of it.
- Ensure that any documents attached as evidence of the wrongdoing (for example, correspondence) are numbered and named (for example, 1-Letter from xx-date, 2-Email to xx-date ...). Please also provide a list with the number and name of each document. Please refer to any document by number in the complaint.
- Please do not provide the Ombudsman with personal data of third parties unless the personal data is necessary to permit the Ombudsman to process the complaint.

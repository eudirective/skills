### Source edition and review policy

Use the responsible official publisher for every source class. Prefer a usable government-published consolidation for national laws and the responsible EU publisher's consolidation for EU laws; if none is available, use the closest usable official version and record its date/cutoff, language, and limits. Do not reconstruct Gazette amendment chains by default. A human-reviewed official link is sufficient for ordinary research; capture, conversion, hashes, and automated updates are optional unless a retained copy or recurring monitoring is needed. Track external-reference usability, local-copy acceptance, topic coverage, and country completion separately. See the [source selection and review policy](references/legal-text-edition-policy.md).

This local library is a derived retrieval workspace. Its expanded core inventory is `core-source-pack-manifest-0.8.0.json` (69 logical records / 70 primary representations; national-language mirrors are indexed separately); [`shared-core-index-EN.json`](shared-core-index-EN.json) maps the 69 English representations to their local files and current retrieval-index hashes. The English index records 46 differences from baseline manifest hashes rather than treating them as unchanged; those local files remain unaccepted artifacts, while their official links can still be reviewed directly. The historical skill bundle remains at 0.6.0 and the protected project `sources/` mirror is unchanged.

Module-scoped research summaries are kept separately in [`../module-notes/`](../module-notes/README.md); they are not authoritative legal texts or hash-pinned source snapshots. ### How the inventory counts work

Counts in this overview use different units and should not be compared as if they were all converted files. An EU **logical record** is a source instrument or case; a **representation** is one language/file for that record. A national pack's **monitored target** is a manifest entry checked by its updater; some targets are discovery-only or monitor-only and do not produce Markdown. A **converted document** is a saved Markdown output. Guidance records are counted in their separate guidance lane. Specifically, Hungary has 58 NJT law references that are human-reviewed only, alongside its separate OIF/NAIH/AJBH maintenance sources; Romania has 79 monitored targets, of which 71 produce Markdown and eight are monitor-only. Language-mirror totals count representations, not additional logical EU instruments.

### National source identities

Every national collection keeps its existing entry IDs and links each row to a canonical `source_identity_id`. The corresponding `representation_ids` identify official references, local artifacts, languages, and editions; several representations may belong to one source. Acceptance, candidate, discovery, historical, and other review states stay attached to the source identity and are reported as overlapping subtotals. Do not infer a merge from similar titles: unresolved identity matches remain separate provisional identities until reviewed. Keep `candidate-manifest.json`, `source-identity-inventory.json`, `source-acceptance.json`, and the managed identity crosswalk in `source-list.md` synchronized with `build-source-identity-inventories.mjs`.

Transient working files, reports, and atomic-write staging files are stored under the project-root `temp/` directory. Its subfolders mirror the source-pack paths; `runtime-storage.mjs` maps those paths to the shared temp tree. Do not keep `captures/`, `pending-updates/`, or unaccepted candidate/discovery files in a country pack. Save those records through the EU Source Update Monitor's local-only archive so its `index.json` remains authoritative for provenance and byte hashes. Accepted Markdown, source inventories, and acceptance decisions remain in their corpus packs.

### Norwegian EEA source crosswalk

Norwegian is not one of the [EU's 24 official languages](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/languages_en). The [NO indexes](national-language-index-NO.json) and [shared-core crosswalk](shared-core-index-NO.json) therefore record EEA counterparts rather than treating Norwegian as a complete EU-language mirror. EFTA EEA-Lex identifies Norwegian EEA Supplement translations for [Directive 2004/38/EC](https://www.efta.int/eea-lex/32004l0038), [Recommendation 2013/461/EU](https://www.efta.int/eea-lex/32013h0461) and [GDPR 2016/679](https://www.efta.int/eea-lex/32016r0679); each is recorded as an official external reference with its Joint Committee Decision, entry-into-force date and Supplement issue/pages. Direct EFTA PDF attempts for the 2004/38 and GDPR issues returned HTTP 403; no local PDFs are claimed, and the SOLVIT issue is linked by EEA-Lex metadata. The [EEA-Lex factsheet for Regulation 2018/1725](https://www.efta.int/eea-lex/32018r1725) says the EEA EFTA States consider it not relevant for incorporation. The other rows are explicit “not verified in this review” or CJEU language dispositions, not findings that a Norwegian source cannot exist. The Directive 2004/38 EEA reference carries a [Joint Declaration attached to JCD 158/2007](https://www.efta.int/sites/default/files/documents/legal-texts/eea/other-legal-documents/adopted-joint-committee-decisions/2007%20-%20English/158-2007-declaration.pdf): the EEA Agreement has no equivalent to EU citizenship and limits the directive's scope, including the separate treatment of immigration. An EEA version or EFTA Court decision is not a substitute for the corresponding EU act or CJEU judgment.

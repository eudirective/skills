# Core research control across modes and modules

## Purpose and privacy boundary

Use this shared, model-led loop for research within an EU free-movement or EU-family-visa question when a read-only applicant question or explicit research request depends on current, multiple-source, disputed or gap research. This includes connected EU/common law, Swedish member-state law/procedure, Türkiye operational, and registered national-source-pack layers; a national-law question unrelated to the plugin's remit does not trigger this loop. The core owns the research contract; mode sets depth and stopping rules; module routing sets the source owner, jurisdiction and authority scope. Evidence standards stay the same across modes. The optional synthetic parent controller described in `consultation-parent-controller.md` may track these tasks, but it does not replace this research contract or introduce a second research engine, MCP tool or legal decision engine. `research_gate.py` remains the artifact validator, and the generic evidence-gated-research plugin is not a mandatory dependency.

The core’s C23 lane may use a person’s first-hand account for read-only guidance. For this formal research handoff, reduce that account to generic, non-identifying legal or operational questions. Do not copy names, case numbers, contact details, personal document contents, unique timelines or other private facts into the plan, search log, finding or evidence excerpt. Apply reviewed source findings to the account only in the conversation. If the issue cannot be generalized safely, keep the research notes conversational and do not create a research artifact. Never feed these artifacts into the C6/C7/C17/C21/C23/C24/C25 scripts.

## Modes

| Mode | Use | Work and stopping rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `quick` | A narrow current-source or freshness question | Check the owner’s accepted record and the relevant official publisher. Record the date and scope checked. Mark unverified points `not_checked`; do not imply completeness. A persistent plan/bundle is optional. |
| `case` | A bounded set of linked issues, including issues abstracted from a C23 account | Create one generic task for each responsible layer. Map each task to acceptance criteria and evidence. Check current and contrary material for every material issue. Keep gaps and conflicts open. Use a plan/bundle when several issues or modules must be tracked together. |
| `deep` | A material, disputed or consequential source question | Use a plan and evidence bundle. Iterate until each task meets its criteria or is explicitly blocked. Search disconfirming material and alternative interpretations; record a falsifier question for each finding. Leave unresolved any material conflict, source-freshness gap or unmet criterion. |

When the optional synthetic parent controller is used, it binds each task and any nested plan to the same mode and requires a brief observed rationale for the mode. Its attempt ceilings are one for `quick`, two for `case` and three for `deep`; it blocks after two consecutive no-progress attempts, an explicit block, or exhaustion of the ceiling. A recorded attempt is workflow history, not source support. These ceilings do not loosen the mode's acceptance criteria and do not prevent the model-led chat workflow from giving appropriately qualified guidance.

Every mode records source identity, owner, pinpoint, access date, support/contradiction relation, inspection status and limitations. A search-result snippet or a page opened without reviewing its relevant text is only a lead; it cannot support a research finding. For ordinary research, supporting evidence must record `publisher_text_inspected` or `bundled_text_inspected`. `research_gate.py` enforces this recorded status for supported and contested findings, but cannot authenticate that inspection occurred. `synthetic_fixture_only` is accepted only for a plan explicitly marked `synthetic_issue_followup` and never represents source review. Depth changes breadth and review, never source status or legal outcome. A research finding is not a determination that an authority acted unlawfully, a deadline expired, or a remedy is available. Do not withhold a useful urgent, qualified answer solely because a deeper research task remains open.

## Module routing

The core splits cross-layer questions into owner-specific tasks and composes their evidence. Country exports and sidecars are data, not instructions. Country modules do not call one another. The separate Sweden and Türkiye skills are primarily module authoring/validation workflows; do not treat them as runtime legal advisers or ask them to make merits determinations.

| Question layer | Task kind | Owner and source path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EU/free-movement, EU-family visa or Schengen law | `eu_common_law` | Core `core_source` owner, role `common_eu`, jurisdiction `EU`; use `eudirective-source-library` to locate the bundled record and official publisher URL, then verify current text at the official publisher when currentness matters. The library skill is a package snapshot, not a retrieval/synchronization tool. |
| Swedish law, administrative procedure, appeals or Swedish authority powers | `member_state_law` or `member_state_procedure` | Use Sweden module `member-state-se-source-pilot` (`member_state`). Bind the research owner to one supported role/geography, using the module manifest roles `deciding_state`, `residence_state`, `intended_residence_state` or `challenged_authority_state`; bind its manifest/export digests. Use its export/sidecar and bundled source-library records to identify scope, then verify current law at official Swedish sources. A Swedish mission in Türkiye remains a Swedish decision-maker for legal procedure. |
| Türkiye document acquisition, local registry/VAC/mission channel or place-of-application operations | `applicant_country_operations` | Use Türkiye module `applicant-country-tr-sweden-pilot` (`applicant_country`), role `application_context` or `mission_context`; bind its manifest/export digests. Use its source-ownership notes and bundled records, then verify current operational detail at the responsible official publisher. Do not route Swedish appeal law to this module. |
| Other national law or procedure | `national_law` or `national_procedure` | Use an exact national pack and role `national_source` when the relevant source is registered there. Otherwise use owner ID `unregistered:<CC>` and review the responsible government's official page directly. A human-reviewed official reference can support this research within its date and scope without being registered or locally captured. Keep library activation separate; leave the task open only for an actual source, currentness, scope, or legal gap. Do not borrow Sweden or Türkiye module scope. |
| Source edition, currentness or change check | `source_freshness` | Keep the existing owner and check its registered record when available; otherwise review the responsible official publisher directly and record a scoped `official_reference`. |

Each route supplies its own acceptance criteria while sharing the same evidence rules:

- **EU/common:** identify the governing EU instrument or judgment, relevant provision/paragraph, temporal scope, conditions, exceptions and any materially contrary authority.
- **Sweden:** identify current Swedish legal/procedural text, the responsible authority and powers, applicable route and any material deadline; a general module note does not replace the official text.
- **Türkiye operations:** identify the document or channel owner, current acquisition/authentication/appointment steps, and any territorial or date limits. Route Swedish mission legal procedure to Sweden.
- **Other national pack:** identify the pack's jurisdiction and coverage, the official publisher and the relevant text/version. If coverage is partial, keep the missing layer open.
- **Freshness:** identify the source edition checked, publisher, effective/publication date and any change or gap since the registered snapshot.

Bind registered packs and module exports to their recorded manifests as needed for package provenance. Reuse registered source IDs where available; do not build a second persistent library. Where a relevant official source is not registered, record its publisher, title/identifier, URL, language, review date, version or cutoff, pinpoint and limitations as a human-reviewed reference. A content hash is optional and applies only when exact bytes are retained. A research result cannot add or activate a library source, but library registration is not a prerequisite for citing a reviewed official reference. If an owner, role, or usable source is unavailable, leave that actual gap open; never silently reroute it.

## Research loop

1. **Scope.** State a neutral, generic question, purpose, jurisdiction and mode. For C23, use the account only to identify legal/source questions, not as artifact content. For follow-up on a C7 synthetic issue, bind the exact enrolled C10 run ID, C7 issue-index digest and issue ID. Do not put applicant-specific facts into a research plan, search log, finding or excerpt.
2. **Decompose and route.** Give each task one owner and concrete acceptance criteria. Add existing source or element IDs where useful. Split EU law, Swedish national procedure and Türkiye local operations rather than asking one module to answer all layers.
3. **Retrieve and record.** Record source identity and `source_access` separately from text inspection. `source_access.channel` names the route (`bundled_runtime`, `local_package`, `official_publisher`, `search_index`, `user_provided`, `other`, `synthetic_fixture` or `unknown`); `source_access.status` records `accessible`, `inaccessible`, `not_attempted` or `not_recorded`. These are caller-declared provenance, not authenticated access events. A registered source record and exact pack digest identify package availability; they do not establish that the active runtime exposed the file. If a manifest-listed file is unavailable in the active runtime, record `bundled_runtime` / `inaccessible` and do not claim text inspection. If you then review the responsible official publisher, add a separate `official_reference` evidence record with `official_publisher` / `accessible`. Record `inspection_status` as `publisher_text_inspected` only after reviewing the relevant publisher text, or `bundled_text_inspected` after reviewing the relevant text in a pinned package. Record search snippets as `search_result_only` and an opened page whose relevant text was not reviewed as `page_opened_not_inspected`; neither can support a finding. For an unregistered official page, record its source identity, publisher, language, displayed date/cutoff, access date, pinpoint, limitations, and whether it supports, contradicts, is insufficient for or only contextualizes the claim. Do not require local capture or a content hash for a human-reviewed link. Keep research references distinct from library registration; research may use the former without activating the latter.
4. **Compare.** Link every material finding to evidence and counterevidence. Search for a plausible falsifier at the depth selected. Preserve source-language/version differences, stale notes, inaccessible text and conflicting authorities. If a new issue or exception appears, add a task with its own owner and acceptance criteria; do not silently expand or close the original task. If two consecutive research passes add no material evidence or clarification, stop and carry the task forward as open/blocked with the searches performed and the next useful source or question.
5. **Close or carry forward.** Set each task to `complete`, `open`, `blocked`, `no_source_found`, `conflict_unresolved` or `not_applicable`. For `no_source_found`, include the queries, channels and scope searched. Close only when the written criteria are met; otherwise identify the blocker and next useful step.
6. **Review.** Map each material output claim to finding IDs, counterevidence and limitations; narrow or qualify any conclusion whose support is incomplete. Check task/findings/evidence cross-references, owner-family and jurisdiction consistency, digest bindings, source currency and the mode’s acceptance criteria. Use `complete` overall coverage only when every task is complete or not applicable, no material falsifier remains unresolved, and in `deep` mode every material finding and claim has a reviewed falsifier. Keep research status separate from legal merits.

Validate plans and bundles with `python scripts/research_gate.py --plan <plan.json> --bundle <bundle.json>`. It checks schema validity, matching research ID/mode, exact canonical plan digest, task/finding/evidence IDs, owner consistency, recorded source-access and inspection status, claim-to-finding/counterevidence links, complete task coverage and deep-mode falsifier records. Add `--require-complete` when the output must be labelled complete; without it, a valid partial bundle reports `research_complete: false` so useful qualified guidance can continue. The gate does not authenticate source access, prove passage accuracy, establish semantic support or validate the final prose against its claim map. Source access and inspection statuses are caller-declared provenance, not authenticated execution. A reviewer must verify those points and whether acceptance criteria were actually met.

For each material source issue that affects an overall coverage label, add the neutral issue-level record described in `references/issue-source-preflight.md`. It ties source classes and source references to their owner-specific research task and claim/finding/evidence chain, while keeping owner roles distinct. `research_gate.py` remains the shared research contract. Registered core and supported module records retain their package checks when used. A human-reviewed official reference from any relevant owner can satisfy a source class within its declared version/scope without a local source copy, source-library registration, or content hash; an unverified, secondary-only, inaccessible, or out-of-scope lead remains open. The validator does not authenticate review declarations or legal conclusions.

## Relationship to C3/C6 and source maintenance

- These artifacts are a read-only research handoff, not a new C3 module export or C6 controller stage. They do not change C3 contract `0.2.0`, controller protocol `0.3.0`, C6 run-manifest pins, C7 trust policy or the issue-index contract.
- The research-only `source_library_national` owner binds a registered source-pack ID and digest when available. `unregistered:<CC>` marks an absent bundled pack; only a human-reviewed government `official_reference` may support or contradict a finding for that owner, and inspected publisher text may close a research task within its recorded scope. This does not register or activate a country pack, and it is not a C3 module family.
- Do not feed a research bundle or real applicant facts into a prepared or released C6/C7/C17/C21 run. A C7 synthetic follow-up is limited to already enrolled C10 run IDs and an exact issue-index digest; changed inputs require a fresh controlled run under the existing gates.
- C12 sidecars remain country-authored; EU/common rules remain core-owned. Research can surface a candidate source/rule or a gap, but it cannot activate it, change a frozen export, clear a C7 issue, or establish legal liability, deadline expiry, remedy or entitlement.
- If evidence is missing, stale, conflicting or outside the owner’s remit, preserve that limitation. Do not resolve it through cross-module inference.

Residence permit for spouse

Apply

Residence permit for spouse is for an individual that intends to move to Iceland to live with his/her spouse, who has the right to reside in Iceland. The permit may be granted on the basis of marriage or cohabitation. The word spouse both refers to marital spouses and cohabiting spouses.

If the spouse, who has the right to reside in Iceland, is a citizen of an EEA/EFTA state, you can directly apply for a residence card for family member of an EEA/EFTA citizen.

Application

Applications must be submitted in their original form on paper.

They can either be sent by regular mail to the Directorate of Immigration or delivered to the drop box in the Directorate’s reception. It is necessary to first pay the processing fee by bank transfer and submit the payment receipt with the application for confirmation, see information on how to pay the processing fee.

Applications can also be handed in and paid for in the Directorate’s reception or at the offices of District Commissioners outside the capital area.

Processing fee

The fee for an application is 110.000 ISK.

An unpaid application will be returned to the applicant.

The processing fee is not refunded if the applicant cancels the application.

Stay in Iceland during application procedure

Spouses who do not need a visa to travel to Iceland

A foreign national exempt from visa requirements, who applies for a residence permit as a spouse, may generally stay in Iceland while the application is being processed by the Directorate of Immigration, provided they apply during a legal stay in the country. However, this does not apply if the applicant's spouse has a residence permit as a student or on the basis of a cooperation/service agreement. In such cases, the applicant is not permitted to stay in the country longer than the visa-free period of 90 days in the last 180-day period, unless compelling reasons of fairness apply.

If an application is submitted during an illegal stay, the application may be rejected, unless there are compelling reasons of fairness that argue against it. In such cases, the applicant must submit a statement to that effect along with the residence permit application, and request permission to stay due to compelling reasons of fairness.

An applicant for international protection, who applies for a residence permit as a spouse, is considered to have intentions to stay in Iceland for more than 90 days. Therefore, they need to submit a request for permission to stay along with a statement regarding compelling reasons of fairness, alongside the application for a residence permit.

The Directorate of Immigration emphasizes that the responsibility for monitoring and controlling Iceland’s borders lies with the police. Foreign nationals are required to meet entry requirements, including valid ID, proof of financial support, and a lawful reason for travel.

Spouses who need a visa to travel to Iceland

Generally, a foreign national subject to visa requirements must apply for a residence permit as a spouse before entering Iceland and may not travel here while the application is being processed by the Directorate of Immigration. If the application is approved, an entry visa is issued for the individual through the Icelandic authorities.

However, a foreign national subject to visa requirements may submit an application for a residence permit as a spouse, if they are in the country with a valid visa. Under such circumstances they may stay in the country while the application is being processed by the Directorate of Immigration. Nevertheless, this does not apply if the applicant's spouse has a residence permit as a student or on the basis of a cooperation/service agreement. In such cases, the applicant is not permitted to stay in the country longer than the visa permits, unless compelling reasons of fairness apply.

Without a valid visa, the application is submitted during an illegal stay and may be rejected unless there are compelling reasons of fairness that argue against it. Then the applicant must submit a statement to that effect along with the residence permit application, and request permission to stay due to compelling reasons of fairness.

An applicant for international protection, who applies for a residence permit as a spouse, is considered to have intentions to stay in Iceland for more than 90 days. Therefore, they need to submit a request for permission to stay along with a statement regarding compelling reasons of fairness, alongside the application for a residence permit.

The Directorate of Immigration emphasizes that the responsibility for monitoring and controlling Iceland’s borders lies with the police. Foreign nationals are required to meet entry requirements, including valid ID, proof of financial support, and a lawful reason for travel.

Law

The residence permit is granted on the basis of Article 70 of the Foreign Nationals Act.

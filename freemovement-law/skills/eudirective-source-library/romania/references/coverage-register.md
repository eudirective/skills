## Adoption route and evidence crosswalk

**Reviewed:** 23 October 2026 against the accepted Portal Legislativ links `ro-portal-232354`, `ro-portal-288410-adoption-rules`, and `ro-portal-55426-foreign-adoption-authorizations`. This is an issue map of those dated references, not a current-law certification, local-copy acceptance, case-specific legal advice, or topic-completion finding. Their displayed cutoffs are 19 April 2024, 19 September 2024, and 12 August 2016, respectively. The HG nr. 1.441/2004 page could not be opened in the research viewer; the official search result and the Law nr. 273/2004 Article 64 footnote establish its identity, title and cited role, but not its full current text.

| Classify the route by ordinary residence | Law 273/2004 arts. 2–4: domestic adoption requires the child and adopter/family each to have ordinary residence in Romania. Intercountry adoption is defined by their ordinary residences being in different states and the child moving to the adopter’s state after approval. | For Romanian citizens (including multiple-nationality citizens), the adopter/family test in art. 3 uses Romanian domicile and actual continuous residence for the six months before the attestation request, subject to its stated temporary-absence exceptions. For EU/EEA citizens and foreigners, art. 3 instead refers to permanent residence/right of permanent stay in Romania. For a child, art. 4 uses Romanian domicile plus actual continuous residence during the 12 months before the adoption-approval application for Romanian citizens; it separately covers EU/EEA citizens and foreigners with permanent residence/right of permanent stay plus the same 12-month period. Rules Annex 1 asks for identity/residence-status documents and an authenticated declaration about the adopter’s six-month residence. | Map evidentiary treatment for each citizenship/residence combination, child’s 12-month residence, absences, residence changes during a case, and proof that a foreign authority or court will accept. The accepted Annex 1 does not itself provide a complete child-residence evidence checklist. |
| Eligibility, consent, and child participation | Law arts. 6–17 address joint adoption, specified bars, moral/material guarantees and consents. Arts. 8–10 set out court treatment of abusive refusal and parent-search situations; arts. 14–16 address timing, advice and the child’s consent. | Parents or guardian are to consent freely and unconditionally after accessible explanation and counseling. A court may overcome an abusive refusal on evidence and the child’s best interests; consecutive non-attendance at two properly summoned consent hearings can count as abusive refusal. If one parent cannot be found after sufficient efforts, the other parent’s consent may suffice; if neither can be found, the law provides for adoption without their consent, with the search report in the file. A child aged 10 or older must consent before the court; their consent is a condition of approval. The child must be heard in specified proceedings, and younger children’s views are sought where possible and assessed by age and maturity. | Map every statutory exception, whose consent is needed in each family-status scenario, the required counseling/search record, parental capacity and age exceptions, and how child views are documented. Do not treat a missing signature alone as proof of valid consent or a valid exception. |
| Domestic applicant assessment and attestation | Law arts. 13, 18–21; Rules arts. 4–16 and Annex 1. The county/sector child-protection directorate at the relevant domicile or an authorized private body conducts assessment; the law considers parenting capacity, health, household, housing, economic situation, motivation and impediments. The final report is due within 90 days; a favorable decision is issued within five working days after that report. | Rules Annex 1 lists identity and residence-status documents, adopter birth/marriage or divorce records, housing title/use evidence, criminal records, income evidence, health and psychiatric assessments, declarations on parental-rights/protection status, and relevant information for a non-joining spouse and co-residents. A negative assessment report may be contested within five working days; the directorate forwards it to an ANPDCA commission, which decides within at most 30 days. | Review the current form models and exact evidence requirements against the applicable route and applicant status. The further remedy, if any, against the commission outcome or a certificate withdrawal/other administrative act is not mapped here. |
| Child adoptability and domestic case file | Law arts. 28–42 and Rules arts. 31–44 and Annex 2 cover the adoption goal in the child’s protection plan, court opening of the adoption procedure, matching and related reports. Law arts. 43–59 govern entrustment and court approval. | Rules Annex 2 identifies the child’s birth certificate, placement/guardianship record, individualized protection plan with adoption as its goal, efforts to identify/find parents, recent protection and family-contact reports, parents’ statements or refusal records, age-14/sibling applicant evidence where relevant, and parental counseling/search reports. Ordinary entrustment is for 90 days; the court approves only when satisfied adoption serves the child’s best interests. | Crosswalk child-status routes (including parent unavailable, family placement, older child, siblings and special-needs cases) to the exact evidence, matching notices, timelines, and reasons for any deviation. Confirm all current forms and case-specific procedural exceptions. |
| Romanian-resident child, adopter/family resident abroad (outgoing intercountry route) | Law arts. 60–74; Rules’ intercountry provisions and Annex 6. Art. 60 limits this route to children in the national authority’s register and specified applicant categories: a relative through the fourth degree; an adopter or one spouse who is a Romanian citizen (subject ordinarily to one year in which no Romanian-resident adopter/family or qualifying relative could be identified); or the child’s parent’s spouse. The one-year wait is bypassed for a qualifying relative and, under art. 60^1, for a registered request for a child aged 14 or older or inseparable siblings where one has reached 14. | Hague-state applications are routed through that state’s Central Authority or accredited body. For a non-Contracting State, art. 64(2) provides a route through its designated adoption authority or an organization accredited there; organizations must also be authorized by the Romanian authority. Art. 65 requires receiving-state evidence of applicant eligibility/suitability, counseling, at least two years’ post-adoption monitoring, post-adoption services, and legal status equivalent to a biological child. Rules Annex 6 lists applicant identity, nationality, birth/marriage records, relationship evidence where relevant, criminal and medical reports, home/social assessment, eligibility certificate, legal-equivalence and services evidence, two-year quarterly monitoring commitment, and counseling evidence. The receiving authority must also confirm continuation and the child’s ability to enter and reside permanently. The selected applicant generally travels to Romania for at least 30 consecutive days of practical matching; the parent-spouse route is exempt from initial and practical matching. The HCCH authority-directory record identifies Romania’s Central Authority and Article 23 competent authority as the National Authority for the Protection of Child’s Rights and Adoption (NAPCRA); those pages were updated 4 June 2026. The linked list of foreign accredited bodies reports its own last update as June 2023. | Separately verify destination-state party relationship, effective dates, declarations/objections and designated authorities for Hague cases. The non-Contracting-State intake channel is identified, but its receiving-state recognition/effects and the operation of Hague-only certificates are not. HG nr. 1.441/2004 is accepted only as a dated link through 12 August 2016 (`ro-portal-55426-foreign-adoption-authorizations`); the viewer did not expose the full text, and the current authorization procedure and authorized-organization list remain open. |
| Romanian-resident adopter, child ordinarily resident abroad (incoming intercountry route) | Law art. 63: the adopter/family application is sent to the competent foreign authorities only through the Romanian central adoption authority; the Romanian applicant’s assessment follows the domestic applicant-assessment provisions in Chapter III. | The Romanian adopter’s assessment and attestation evidence follows the domestic route above. Child adoptability and dossier requirements are governed in material part by the child’s state of ordinary residence; the source reviewed here does not provide that foreign state’s evidence rules. | Identify the foreign state’s central authority, consent/adoptability materials, applicable convention relationship, Romanian court or recognition route, and the evidence required to establish the adoption’s effects in Romania. |
| Court jurisdiction, procedure, and appeal | Law arts. 61, 72, 84–92. Romanian courts have jurisdiction if at least one party is ordinarily resident in Romania and exclusive jurisdiction where a Romanian-citizen or stateless child is ordinarily resident in Romania. Adoption applications go to the tribunal for the adoptee’s domicile; statutory cases are fee-exempt and heard by specialized panels in chambers with the prosecutor. | The national authority transmits the outgoing intercountry approval petition after prior administrative steps. The statute sets a single appeal: generally 10 days; 30 days for the decision opening the adoption procedure. A party or prosecutor may appeal; appeals are heard urgently and with priority. The child aged 10 or older is heard in specified proceedings and must consent at approval. | Map the start point for each appeal deadline, service and participation requirements, interim protection, contested administrative-stage decisions, and any distinct review of adoption-register or authority action. These statutory adoption appeals do not by themselves resolve remedies against every administrative decision. |
| Hague recognition, non-Convention recognition, travel, and post-adoption | Law arts. 62, 65, 73–74 and 95–100; shared-core treaty records `hcch:convention-33` and `hcch:convention-33-status`. The HCCH Convention applies when the child and receiving state meet its scope; art. 23 recognition in another Contracting State depends on certification that the adoption complied with the Convention, and art. 24 preserves a narrow public-policy refusal. Romania’s HCCH status-table row records signature on 29 May 1993, ratification on 28 December 1994 and entry into force on 1 May 1995; the table is dated 17 June 2025. | Law art. 73 provides for a Romanian Hague-conformity certificate after the final approval judgment; art. 74 permits travel only after finality and requires the child to travel accompanied by the adopter or at least one adopting spouse. Law art. 65 requires receiving-state guarantees for equal legal status and at least two years’ monitoring/services. Law art. 62 refers citizenship effects to Law nr. 21/1991. Domestic-adoption support benefits under arts. 100^1–100^6 are not a general intercountry benefit route; the separate Article 83(1) annulment record is limited to its support-allowance context. | For a Hague case, check both states’ effective treaty relationship for the application date, any acceptance of an accession, declarations and designated competent/certifying authorities. For a non-Contracting State, identify its own recognition, status, nationality and travel rules; do not infer automatic recognition from Romania’s intake channel or from a Romanian judgment. Reconcile the Citizenship Law dependency and review post-adoption reports/forms. |

This crosswalk narrows the open work but does not close the adoption topic. In particular, the receiving-state rules, exact case-specific evidence, current administrative forms, review of non-court decisions, and post-cutoff amendments remain unverified. The 2025 ÎCCJ Decision nr. 326/2025 remains a narrow status reference concerning the annulled methodological-rules Article 83(1) in the described support-allowance context, not a general adoption holding.

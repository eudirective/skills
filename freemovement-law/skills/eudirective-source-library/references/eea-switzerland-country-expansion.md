# EEA and Switzerland national-source expansion

Status date: 21 October 2026
Sequence: alphabetical, one jurisdiction at a time  
Target: 31 jurisdictions — the 27 EU Member States, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland. Switzerland is included by request but is not an EEA Contracting Party.

The EU list is confirmed by the [European Union's country directory](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/eu-countries_en). EFTA identifies Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway as the three EEA EFTA States and expressly distinguishes Switzerland from the EEA Agreement ([EEA EFTA States](https://www.efta.int/eea-relations-eu/eea-institutions-two-pillar-structure/eea-efta-states)).

## Comparison baseline

Use the existing Denmark, Sweden, Czechia, Hungary, and Romania packs and their prior expansion findings as leads, not as a claim that each state has an equivalent instrument. For each jurisdiction, search and record these source classes separately:

1. National rules and official guidance for EEA/EU/Swiss free movement and third-country family members, including visas, entry, residence documents, and competent authorities.
2. Administrative procedure, evidence, file access, language, and time limits.
3. Appeals, review of delay, ombudsmen, complaints, and data-protection remedies.
4. Official application forms, consular instructions, public authority policies, and relevant court or oversight material.

Record functional relationship, publisher verification, search outcome, legal status/version evidence, and any optional retrieval route separately. “Accepted official reference” means a trustworthy government-published link reviewed for its stated use, language, version or cutoff, and limits. “Accepted local copy,” “topic adequately covered,” and “country review complete” are separate outcomes. A capture failure or missing updater does not block use of a trustworthy official link, and a source link does not prove coverage completeness.

## Source review expectations per jurisdiction

- Keep a dated inventory of known source leads, the official publisher, language, scope, and material search gaps.
- For ordinary research, a human-reviewed official page or document link is sufficient within the identity, language, date/version or cutoff, passage, and caveats actually checked. Prefer a usable government-published consolidation; if none is available, use the closest government-published version and record its limits. Do not reconstruct Gazette amendment chains by default.
- A source does not need a custom retrieval or conversion rule to be usable as an official reference. Record a local artifact as accepted only when it has actually been retained and reviewed for its intended use; that status does not determine topic coverage or country completion.
- Document payload, conversion, replay, failure, reuse, and change-detection details only for a retained local copy or an automated monitoring workflow that is actually planned. Scale checks to the passages and attachments relied on.
- Keep material search gaps and access failures visible. API registration, bulk retrieval, full-document conversion, and scheduled updating are not prerequisites for link-based research.

## Previous findings to carry forward

- The prior Denmark-to-Sweden/Finland smoke test found Swedish legislation as a partial functional match and left the Finnish Finlex and Migri candidates only provisionally verified because direct inspection failed. Austria is searched from the same Danish free-movement seed, but its NAG is a broad statute with an EEA-specific section cluster rather than a dedicated regulation; this is a partial functional match, not an equivalent instrument.
- The source taxonomy review identified country-level gaps around administrative procedure, file access, consular/mission practice, family-status evidence, remedies, privacy/data access, and operational guidance. Its Germany examples were leads, not proof of completeness or instructions to limit coverage to Germany.
- Czechia's registration-gated e-Sbírka REST API was not used. A public unauthenticated frontend page-data route captured the identified current texts; because its schema is undocumented, carry forward the lesson to distinguish official public access from a stable, documented integration contract.

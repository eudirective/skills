# Belgium source discovery register

Research date: 18 October 2026  
Scope: Belgian national, federal, regional, mission, court, and oversight sources related to EU/EEA/Swiss free movement and third-country family members, entry and residence, administration, remedies, privacy, and access to records. The comparison leads were the Denmark, Sweden, Czechia, Hungary, and Romania source reviews. Belgium is an EU Member State; its national routes are checked independently.

This is a bounded discovery register, not an accepted source pack or a complete inventory of Belgian law. It contains 39 distinct candidates and 0 accepted sources, including two previously retrieved and converted review artifacts. Each candidate is a lead or related source, not a claim of legal equivalence. The machine-readable record is candidate-manifest.json.

## Search and coverage notes

- Official first-party routes checked included Justel, the Immigration Office (IBZ), FPS Foreign Affairs and mission sites, the Federal Ombudsman, the Data Protection Authority, Wallex, the Flemish Codex, Brussels regional government, and the Council for Alien Law Litigation. N-Lex was used only as an EU discovery route.
- French and Dutch publisher routes were used for federal and regional materials. Search and cross-checking included the source titles, legal identifiers, and topic-specific terminology surfaced by the official indexes. A complete raw query transcript was not retained in the source review; that limits reproducibility and should be added before implementing a search-driven adapter.
- A bounded German-language search did not verify an official access-to-records source for the German-speaking Community. This is an incomplete search outcome, not evidence that no source exists.
- Only the UK and UAE mission pages were reviewed. The national competence matrix is a routing source, not coverage of every applicant-residence country, local checklist, or outsourced provider.
- The federal access-to-records law does not settle regional, community, and municipal coverage. Walloon, Flemish, and Brussels routes are separate; German-speaking Community coverage remains open.
- Direct RvV/CCE site and sample-PDF access returned HTTP 403. The selected monthly publication is selective and cannot establish comprehensive case-law coverage.
- The earlier public-mirror inventory contained five candidate records, including two converted review artifacts. The 1980 Law and 1981 Decree records are reconciled here with corrected current identifiers; their former IDs are retained as manifest aliases. The older Dutch Private International Law record, IBZ family page, and Federal Justice adoption guidance remain distinct records.
- Annex 19ter's current index-linked PDF endpoint returned HTML error content. A current valid payload remains unverified.
- The personal-data processing law of 30 July 2018 and additional civil-status/adoption records were not directly verified. The Private International Law Code is a limited lead for foreign public records, not a substitute for those searches.
- The official routes reviewed did not establish a Belgian API, registration route, or programmatic bulk-use permission.

## Scope of conclusions

The official records show useful starting points for immigration legislation, forms, national guidance, consular routing, administrative review, privacy complaints, adoption guidance, and regional access-to-records rules. They do not establish legal equivalence with the Danish seed, current completeness across all Belgian institutions, or a sustainable automated monitor. All 39 records remain candidates pending source-owner and legal-scope review.

Right of entry and residence

A citizen of the European Union (EU citizen) is a foreign national who has the nationality of one of the European Union Member States or one of the associated States of the European Union (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland).

In accordance with Article 41 of the Law of 15 December 1980 and Article 46 of the Royal Decree of 8 October 1981, the EU citizenship is established based on one of the following documents:

A valid ID card or passport; or

An expired ID card or passport; or

Any other proof of identity and nationality.

Under specific conditions, any EU citizen has the right to freely enter and stay on the territory of a Member State of which he/she is not a national.

Warning! As of 3 August 2026, identity cards issued to their nationals by the Member States of the European Union, as well as by Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, must contain a machine-readable zone (MRZ) that complies with the requirements set out in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2025/1208.

Entry in Belgium

The right of entry in Belgium shall be granted to a foreign national who demonstrates that he or she is an EU citizen.

A foreign national who claims to be an EU citizen but is unable to present the documents required to prove it, may be denied entry by the authorities responsible for border control. However, before refusing entry to the person, the Immigration Office shall grant him or her a period within which he or she may present one of those documents.

Good to know: The Immigration Office may impose an administrative fineof 200 euros on an EU citizen who fails to present a valid identity card or passport when presenting himself or herself at the external borders of the European Union.

Short stay in Belgium (less than 3 months)

Reporting presence to the municipality​

An EU citizen coming to Belgium for a short stay must report his or her presence to the municipal administration of the place where he or she is staying within 10 working days of entering Belgium.

The municipal administration shall issue a declaration of presence free of charge. (Annex 3ter). This declaration of presence is not a residence permit.

Good to know!The Immigration Office may impose an administrative fine of 200 euros on an EU citizen who fails to report his or her presence within the 10-day period referred to above.

Exemption

An exemption from the obligation to report one's presence shall be granted to EU citizens who:

are staying in an accommodation that falls under the legislation on checking the details of all foreign guests;

are admitted for treatment in a hospital or similar nursing facility during their stay in Belgium;

have been arrested and detained in a prison or a social welfare establishment.

My address in Belgium

Since 10 April 2026, an EU citizen may provide the address at which he or she will be staying online via the “My Address in Belgium” (MAB) application.

For more information, see the “My Address in Belgium” page

Long stay in Belgium (more than 3 months)

[Provisions in force since 1 September 2025]

Right to stay in Belgium for more than 3 months

[Article 40, §4, of the Law of 15 December 1980 and Article 50, §2, of the Royal Decree of 8 October 1981]

An EU citizen has the right to stay in Belgium for more than 3 months if he/she meets one of the following conditions:

He/she is an employee or a self-employed person in Belgium; or

He/she has entered Belgium to seek employment and proves that he/she is seeking further employment and has a good chance of being hired; or

He/she has sufficient means of subsistence to avoid becoming a burden on the social security system in Belgium during his/her stay, and has health insurance covering all risks in Belgium; or

He/she is enrolled at an organized, recognized or subsidized educational institution for the main purpose of following a course of study, including vocational training, he/she has health insurance covering all risks in Belgium and has sufficient means of subsistence not to become a burden on the social security system in Belgium during his/her stay;

He/she is an EU citizen and he/she is a member of the family - in accordance with Article 40bis, §2 of the Law of 15 December 1980 - of another EU citizen who is entitled to stay in Belgium for more than 3 months.

However, the recognition of this right is subject to conditions. (See below)

Application for a registration certificate (Annex 19)

EU citizens wishing to stay in Belgium for more than three months must submit an application for a registration certificate (Annex 19) to the municipal authority of their place of residence, at the latest at the expiry of the three-month period from the date of arrival in Belgium.

Good to know: The Immigration Office may impose an administrative fine of 200 euros on EU citizens who do not apply for a registration certificate before the expiry of the three-month period.

The municipal authority accepts the application for a registration certificate of the EU citizen if, when he/she submits his/her application, the EU citizen proves he/she is a EU citizen and he/she submits all the documents proving the status under which he/she wishes to stay in Belgium for more than 3 months (he/she is employed, he/she has sufficient means of subsistence, etc.).

The municipal authority will disregard the application for a registration certificate of EU citizens who do not prove they are EU citizens and will issue an Annex 19 quinquies.

If the applicant proves that he/she is an EU citizen but does not submit all documents proving the status under which he/she wishes to stay in Belgium for more than 3 months, the municipal authority disregards his/her application for a registration certificate and issues an Annex 19quinquies. It mentions the missing documents on Annex 19quinquies.

Good to know: an EU citizen can submit a new application for a registration certificate at any time if he/she can submit all the documents proving he/she has the right to stay in Belgium for more than 3 months.

Recognition of the right to stay in Belgium for more than three months

The municipal authority can recognize the right to stay of an EU citizen who submits, when he/she applies for a registration certificate (Annex 19), all documents establishing his/her status of:

Employee or self-employed person; or

Student; or

Holder of sufficient means of subsistence, insofar these means of subsistence are provided by means of an invalidity allowance, an early retirement pension, an old-age allowance or an allowance from the insurance against accidents at work or occupational diseases; or

Spouse or partner in the context of a partnership considered to be equivalent to marriage; or

Descendant under 21 years old.

If the municipal authority recognizes the right to stay of the EU citizen, it gives him/her a provisional document proving his/her registration (Annex 8ter). Then, the municipal authority gives him/her a document proving his/her registration (EU card).

In case of doubt or if the municipal authority is not allowed to recognize the right to stay of an EU citizen, it forwards the file to the Immigration Office.

The Immigration Office must take its decision at the latest 6 months after the date of submission of the application for a registration certificate (Annex 19).

If the Immigration Office recognizes the right to stay of the EU citizen, the municipal authority gives him/her a provisional document proving his/her registration (Annex 8ter). Then, the municipal authority gives him/her a document proving his/her registration (EU card).

If the Immigration Office does not recognize the right to stay of the EU citizen, it denies his/her application for a registration certificate and orders him/her to leave the territory, if applicable (Annex 20).

Warning! The decision to deny an application for a registration certificate is always taken by the Immigration Office (Annex 20).

Right of permanent residence

Duration of stay

​

An EU citizen who has resided in Belgium for an uninterrupted period of five years, starting from their registration in the waiting list register, can request a document proving the permanence of his or her stay from the municipal authority of their place of residence (annex 22).

​

Admissibility of the application for permanent residence

The municipal administration shall declare inadmissible (Annex 23) the application of an EU citizen who has not resided in Belgium for at least five years from the date of his or her registration in the waiting register and who does not submit documents demonstrating that he or she qualifies for one of the derogations provided for in Article 42sexies, § 1 , of the Law of 15 December 1980.

If the EU citizen invokes one of the derogations provided for in Article 42sexies, § 1 of the Law, the municipal administration shall forward the application to the Immigration Office. The Immigration Office must take a decision within five months from the date on which the municipal administration issued Annex 22.​

Examination period

If the application is admissible, the municipal administration may take the decision. However, in the event of doubt, it may forward the application to the Immigration Office. The Immigration Office must take a decision within five months from the date on which the municipal administration issued Annex 22.​

Recognition of the right of permanent residence

Once the right of permanent residence has been recognised (by the municipal administration or by the Immigration Office), the municipal administration shall issue the EU citizen with a provisional document certifying permanent residence. [Annex 8quater to the Royal Decree of 8 October 1981] The municipal administration shall subsequently issue the EU citizen with a document attesting permanent residence. [EU+ Card]

If the right of permanent residence is not recognised, the municipal administration shall notify the EU citizen of the decision of the Immigration Office. [Annex 24 to the Royal Decree of 8 October 1981]

Provisional residence document

If the EU citizen's residence document expires while his or her application for permanent residence is being examined, the municipal administration shall issue him or her with a provisional residence document, the validity of which shall correspond to the remaining portion of the five-month period within which a decision on whether or not to recognise the right of permanent residence must be taken.

Temporary absence

Continuity of residence shall not be affected by temporary absences not exceeding a total of six months per year, by longer absences for the performance of military obligations, or by a single absence of no more than twelve consecutive months for important reasons such as pregnancy and childbirth, serious illness, study or vocational training, or a posting outside the Kingdom for professional reasons.

Loss of the right of permanent residence

Once acquired, the right of permanent residence shall be lost only through absences from Belgium exceeding two consecutive years.

End of stay

The Immigration Office may terminate the residence of an EU citizen who no longer fulfils the conditions for the right to reside in Belgium for more than three months.

The Immigration Office may also terminate the residence of an EU citizen who constitutes an unreasonable burden on Belgium's social security system. In order to determine whether an EU citizen constitutes an unreasonable burden on the social security system, account shall be taken of the temporary nature of his/her difficulties, the duration of his/her stay in Belgium, his/her personal situation and the amount of aid granted.

The Immigration Office can also terminate the residence of an EU citizen who has used false or misleading information, or false or falsified documents, , or who has committed fraud or used other illegal means that have contributed to obtaining residence.

Finally, the Immigration Office can terminate the stay of an EU citizen for reasons of public order, national security or public health.

In taking a decision concerning an EU citizen, the Immigration Office shall take account of the duration of his or her stay in Belgium, his or her age, state of health, family and economic situation, social and cultural integration in Belgium, and the strength of his or her ties with the country of origin.

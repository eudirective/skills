# Croatia procedural and remedies crosswalk

**Reviewed:** 24 October 2026
**State:** preliminary primary-text map; immigration-specific application is unresolved.

This crosswalk starts from the problems a person may need to raise: an excessive evidence demand, failure to hear or consider relevant facts, a late decision, an adverse decision, a defective service, lack of interim protection, or failure to execute a judgment. It records what accepted primary law and current official procedures establish, then marks immigration-specific application questions separately. The EEA Act is the specialist law for EEA nationals and their family members. Some short-stay visa procedures in the Foreigners Act and Visa Code also appear relevant to third-country family members, but must be read subject to the EEA Act and Directive 2004/38. Do not use the Foreigners Act as a substitute for the specialist EEA residence route or assume that every third-country immigration condition applies.

## Issue-led route findings

### Statutory scope and Croatian-national cases

The EEA Act is an express national transposition of Directive 2004/38 (Article 2). Its scope clause covers entry, movement, residence and work of EEA citizens and their family members (Article 1(1)); it also extends the relevant provisions to Swiss citizens and their family members (Article 1(3)), and extends family-member rules to family members of Croatian citizens who are themselves EEA citizens (Article 1(4)). The Act's express treatment of family members of Croatian nationals does not by itself answer the position of a third-country-national family member of a Croatian citizen returning after exercising free movement elsewhere. That route, and the relationship to the Foreigners Act, must be checked against the Directive's cross-border scope and relevant EU case law. Keep it open rather than treating the ordinary third-country residence route as an automatic substitute.

### EEA-family visa, border entry and residence-card decisions

The current official [MVEP EEA/Swiss family-visa page](https://mvep.gov.hr/consular-information-246396/family-members-of-eu-eea-citizens-246399/246399) states that a non-EEA family member travelling with or joining an EEA/Swiss citizen may use a valid residence card for visa-free travel; where a C visa is needed, MVEP describes a priority appointment at the embassy/consulate, expedited processing, no visa fee, and a minimum set of four documents. The [Croatian-language counterpart](https://mvep.gov.hr/konzularne-informacije-244593/konzularne-informacije-22730/vize-22733/clanovi-obitelji-gradjana-egp-a-svicarske-vize/22748) confirms that this facilitation depends on the EEA/Swiss citizen having exercised free movement. The current English page is reviewed as a usable official link within its stated scope; its retired 180456 page ID is superseded. The page is undated and its embedded Directive link is obsolete HTTP, so confirm legal rights against current primary law and mission practice. No local page copy is accepted.

The primary-law route is more specific at the border. Directive 2004/38 Article 5(2) provides the visa/card framework and requires a free, accelerated procedure. EEA Act Article 8(1) recognizes a qualifying family member's valid residence card; Article 8(4)–(5) requires a free accelerated border visa for a qualifying accompanying/joining family member without a valid visa. Article 9(3) and Article 23(7) exclude an ordinary administrative appeal for the specified entry/refusal and residence-card decisions but permit an administrative dispute. This route must be kept separate from a prior consular visa application and its refusal appeal.

The current [MVEP visa-appeal page](https://mvep.gov.hr/informacije-za-gradjane-244593/konzularne-informacije-22730/vize-22733/zalba-protiv-odluke-o-odbijanju-ukidanju-ili-ponistenju-vize-22751/22751) states that an appeal is filed through the competent Croatian embassy/consulate within 15 days of service, normally in Croatian; it gives an English exception if no authorized Croatian court interpreter is available in the recipient state. It says MVEP's Visa Service decides within 60 days, lists an administrative appeal charge of €130 or USD 150.71, and identifies a 30-day court claim route to the Zagreb Administrative Court after the second-instance decision. Foreigners Act Article 32(3)–(6) supplies the refusal notice, 15-day appeal route, responsible ministry and non-suspensive effect. The current MUP index lists the Act chain through NN 55/26, which the MVEP page also cites. The official link is usable within its recorded scope. No local page copy is retained; applicability of the €130 fee to qualifying EEA-family members remains unresolved.

Both operational pages were checked against their displayed content and are usable official links within their recorded scopes. Their exact source responses remain in the Monitor local-only archive; no general page-content reuse basis is asserted. The family page is undated and contains an obsolete embedded Directive URL. The MVEP Article 32/appeal route is an official source lead, but whether the €130 appeal charge applies to qualifying Directive 2004/38 family members, and whether waiver/legal aid is available, remains unresolved. Do not infer an appeal-fee exemption from the visa-issuance fee exemption.

## Accepted law and version basis

- The GAPA source chain is NN 47/09 plus amendment NN 110/21, effective from 1 January 2022. Both texts are separately captured and accepted; the official Ministry and court listings identify this chain. This pack does not claim to hold an official consolidated text.
- The Foreigners Act chain is NN 133/20, 114/22, 151/22, 40/25 and 55/26. All five text records are accepted; it remains distinct from the dedicated EEA-family residence regime.
- The ADA is NN 36/24, effective from 1 July 2024; it replaced the prior Administrative Disputes Act. The official court listings identify NN 36/24. The full text is captured and accepted.
- Official records: [GAPA NN 47/09](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2009_04_47_1065.html), [GAPA amendment NN 110/21](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2021_10_110_1930.html), [ADA NN 36/24](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2024_03_36_564.html).

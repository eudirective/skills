# Hungary source-rule sustainability assessment (2026-10-18)

## Scope and evidence

Assessed the local Hungary pack at manifest version `0.3.0`: `references/manifest.json`, `references/source-list.md`, `SKILL.md`, `README.md`, and `scripts/sync-hungary.mjs`; the shared runtime storage helper and pinned converter dependencies; the current runtime state metadata; and Hungary leads in the 15 October 2026 acquisition register. No source sync, download, conversion, or replay was run.

The manifest contains 58 active NJT law records and 44 active OIF/NAIH/AJBH records. It has 18 discovery records, three of which expand into three yearly URLs, plus three pages marked `discovery_only`: 27 catalogue requests in a routine non-offline run. The active AJBH 2025 complaint PDF is deliberately blocked by an identity-validation guard. Counts describe this local manifest, not the publishers' complete source universe.

Publisher evidence checked live: NJT's [terms of use](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/felhasznalasi_feltetelek) and law-search page; OIF's [public-information register](https://oif.gov.hu/index.php/tajekoztatok/tevekenysegre-mukodesre-vonatkozo-adatok) and EEA registration guidance; NAIH's [online procedures](https://www.naih.hu/online-ugyinditas); the Kúria [decision-search page](https://kuria-birosag.hu/hu/kuriai-dontesek-kereses); the national courts' [anonymized-decision search](https://eakta.birosag.hu/anonimizalt-hatarozatok); and the official [Magyar Közlöny help page](https://magyarkozlony.hu/segitseg). The NAIH, AJBH and court portals' current automated-reuse terms were not established. The prior recorded 403 for one AJBH PDF was not retried.

Runtime state metadata was inspected separately: it was last checked on 11 October 2026 and contained 99 source rows against 102 currently active manifest entries. This is an operational freshness/integrity observation only; it does not affect the rule verdicts below.

## Summary

The Hungary pack is **not sustainable end to end**. The central legislation rule is **unsustainable** under NJT's published restrictions: the workflow retrieves and stores consolidated NJT text in a local database, and the terms prohibit bulk downloading/processing and using downloaded portal content to build a database. The same shared run also requests NJT catalogues. No written permission or documented machine-access agreement is recorded.

The OIF rule is **fragile**: it uses official parent pages and stable-looking direct links, but many records lack source-specific identity checks, PDFs receive no OCR quality review, and each ordinary run makes up to 128 requests across the pack without request timeouts, backoff, or source-specific host restrictions. NAIH and AJBH rules have similar identity and access uncertainties. Court discovery remains a lead only, with weak automatic coverage of query-driven results.

This is a source-rule assessment, not a finding on legal force or current law. It does not assess GitHub mirror synchronization.

## Cross-cutting design risks

- A regular online mode fetches up to 101 active payloads (one AJBH record stops at its guard) and 27 catalogue pages: up to 128 HTTP requests in one run. It has no request timeout, retry/backoff, 429 handling, or inter-request delay.
- `fetchOfficial` permits redirects to any hostname in the pack-wide list of ten official hosts instead of requiring the source's expected host or an explicit alias set.
- `TextDecoder` is non-fatal by default, so malformed UTF-8 can be replaced silently. The HTML decoder supports only a selected set of named entities.
- The sync path saves successful records if another record fails and reports partial success; the blocked AJBH record is nevertheless included in the active set, so it makes a clean full-source run impossible until reclassified or resolved.
- `--discover` first loops over every active source before fetching catalogue pages. It therefore cannot be used as a low-volume catalogue-only operation and cannot isolate the prohibited NJT route.

## Repair priorities

1. **Stop automated NJT fetching and NJT catalogue polling unless written permission is obtained.** The law texts' public-domain status does not override the portal terms on the curated database. Acceptance condition: either written terms explicitly cover internal automated retrieval, storage and conversion, or the automated NJT adapter is removed from scheduled/manual runner modes.
2. **Use a no-registration, human-reviewed Gazette workflow as the interim legal-source workaround.** Magyar Közlöny help documents full-text/date search and signed issue PDFs from 3 May 2013 onward; it also warns that full-issue downloads generate significant traffic. For each registered law, a researcher can pin its official enactment issue and manually review later Gazette issues for amendment candidates, recording the issue number, page/article, publication date and effective-date note. Keep the consolidated NJT page as an on-demand human reference only; do not copy it into the pack. This is a proposed workflow, not an implemented or sustainability-rated rule. Before automating even issue-page discovery or PDF retrieval, verify the Gazette's terms and permitted request volume.
3. **Decouple adapters and run modes.** Allow OIF/NAIH/AJBH checks and catalogue discovery without requesting NJT sources; add per-publisher pacing, finite request timeouts, 429 handling, expected-host redirect rules and strict character decoding. Acceptance condition: a failure or blocked publisher does not trigger requests to unrelated publishers or mark a fresh check.
4. **Strengthen source identity and PDF conversion.** Add an expected title, publisher, record number and visible edition/date marker for each active page/PDF. For scanned PDFs, record OCR confidence and require reviewer inspection below a threshold. Acceptance condition: wrong-page, wrong-document, partial-page, and malformed-encoding fixtures fail closed; reviewer comparison confirms tables, article numbering, footnotes and annexes.
5. **Resolve or reclassify `ajbh-general-complaint-submission`.** Do not keep a permanently blocked unresolved record in the active retrieval set. Acceptance condition: access is confirmed, a first-party identity marker is verified, and the publisher's conditions are recorded; otherwise keep it discovery-only.
6. **Repair identifiers and carry forward the uncovered civil-status lead.** `hu-act-2016-100` points to citation 2016. évi CL. while its route is `/eli/TV/2016/150`; align the record ID. The 15 October acquisition register identified Act I of 2010 on civil registration as a relevant `FAMILY-04/28/27` lead, but that act is absent from the current 58-law manifest. Verify and record it as a candidate; do not add a retrieval rule until a permitted source route is established.
7. **Recheck the prior Act CL/2016 conversion finding.** The 15 October register noted navigation artifacts in its converted text. The current converter targets NJT's legal-content container, but no saved-output comparison or replay was performed in this audit. Acceptance condition: compare the full converted text against the official human-viewed source and retain only the intended body.

## Separate coverage limits

The pack includes free-movement Act I/2007 and Decree 113/2007, but the earlier civil-registration lead is missing from its active list. Its supporting-law classes are broad and selected; this assessment does not prove that every family-status, local, mission, case-law, or administrative-procedure source has been identified. Kúria and Eakta entries are discovery pages, not a court-decision corpus. The unresolved AJBH booklet is not a usable source. No legal-currentness, completeness, or legal-equivalence conclusion is made.

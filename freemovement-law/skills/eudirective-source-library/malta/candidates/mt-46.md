It gives me great pleasure to present to you the first ever 
compiled Protocol Guide for Diplomats and Diplomatic Missions 
accredited to Malta. This Guide is intended for the use of all 
diplomatic missions accredited to Malta, as well as International 
Organisations that have a seat agreement with Malta. 
The Protocol Directorate within the Ministry responsible for 
Foreign Affairs is the first point of contact for all diplomatic 
missions. In this context this document is intended to provide 
guidance on the different procedures to be followed when 
dealing with the Protocol Directorate.
The Guide is intended to answer most queries raised by 
diplomatic missions and international organisations. It lays 
out the diplomatic privileges and immunities accorded by the 
Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) and how 
these are implemented correctly in Malta. 
This Protocol Guide shall be updated as necessary and is 
provided for information purposes only. It neither constitutes 
an authoritative legal statement of any kind nor commits the 
Government of Malta in any way.
I do hope that you will find this Protocol Guide useful throughout 
your diplomatic posting in Malta
Kerstin Spiteri
Director Protocol

In this Guide of Diplomatic Immunities and Privileges in Malta, unless otherwise indicated:
“consular officer” means any person appointed as consul-general, consul, vice-consul or consular 
agent by a foreign State and holding a valid exequatur or other authorization to act in Malta in 
that capacity;
“consular post” means any consulate-general, consulate, vice-consulate or consular agency;
“diplomatic agent” is the head of the mission or a member of the diplomatic staff of the mission;
“diplomatic mission” means an embassy or a high commission or any other entity as may be 
approved as a diplomatic entity by the Minister responsible for Foreign Affairs;
“head of mission” is the person charged by the sending State with the duty of acting in that 
capacity;
“international organisation” means an organisation, institute, agency, a regional office or branch 
of an international organisation or any other entity, whose status in Malta is governed by a host 
country agreement;
“members of the mission” are the head of mission and the members of the staff of the mission;
“members of the staff of the mission” are the members of the diplomatic staff, of the administrative 
and technical staff and of the service staff of the mission;
“members of the diplomatic staff” are the members of the staff of the mission having diplomatic 
rank;
“members of the administrative and technical staff” are the members of the staff of the mission 
employed in the administrative and technical service of the mission;
“members of the service staff” are the members of the staff of the mission in the domestic service 
of the mission;
Interpretation

<!-- page 4 -->
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Foreign Affairs;
“Ministry” means the Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs;
“official residence” means the residence of an Ambassador, a High Commissioner or a Head of an 
international organisation;
“private servant” is a person who is in the domestic service of a member of the mission and who 
is not an employee of the sending State;
“premises of the mission” are the buildings or parts of buildings and the land ancillary thereto, 
irrespective of ownership, used for the purposes of the mission including the official residence of 
the head of mission;
“resident official” means an official residing in Malta who is accredited either to a diplomatic 
mission, a consular post or an international organisation and who is in possession of a current 
identity card issued by the Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs and who falls within the 
categories listed in the relative provisions of the Diplomatic Immunities and Privileges Act 
(Chapter 191 of the Laws of Malta), but excludes an official who is a Maltese national or a person 
recruited locally who is ordinarily resident in Malta.
The term “ambassador” unless otherwise indicated also refers to High Commissioner.

The sending State must ascertain that an agrément has been obtained to the person proposed 
to be accredited as an Ambassador to Malta. The sending State shall address its request for an 
agrément to the Protocol Directorate, Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs, through a Note 
Verbale, enclosing a curriculum vitae in English and a passport copy (diplomatic or national). The 
request for an agrément is to be sent not less than two months prior to the intended date of 
arrival in Malta. This will allow the Maltese authorities to make the necessary procedures before 
an agrément can be issued.
Once the agrément has been received, the sending State may accredit the designated Ambassador 
as its representative to Malta, and procedures for arrival in Malta can be initiated. A non-resident 
Ambassador may seek agrément according to the same procedure as a resident Ambassador. 
However, a non-resident Ambassador must obtain agrément in the country where he/she has 
official residence before requesting agrément in Malta.
1.1  Agrément
The date and flight details of arrival of the new Ambassador must be notified to the Protocol 
Directorate by a Note Verbale.
On arrival, the new Ambassador will be met at the Malta International Airport - Ministerial Lounge 
- by a representative of the Protocol Directorate, from Monday to Friday between 0800 and 2000 
hrs, who conveys the greetings of the Minister.
The Ministerial lounge will only be arranged for a Head of Mission in connection with the first 
arrival in Malta and last departure following the farewell calls. 
It should be noted that a new Head of Mission can only arrive in Malta after the previous Head of 
Mission would have departed Malta.
1.2 Arrival of a new Head of Mission
On the first courtesy call on the Minister responsible for Foreign Affairs, the designated Ambassador 
is to present a copy of the Letters of Credence. Once such letters have been presented, the 
designated Ambassador is free to enter into negotiations with the Minister.
In case the Minister responsible for Foreign Affairs is unavailable, the Ambassador will present 
the copie d’usage to the Permanent Secretary of the Ministry for Foreign Affairs or the Director 
Protocol.

1.7.2 Transport 
All transportation arrangements in relation to the appointments on the Official Programme of 
non-resident Ambassadors, when travelling to present their Letters of Credence, are made and 
borne by the Ministry. This does not include any additional meetings which the Ambassador 
requests. In these cases, the Embassy is to organize transportation for any additional meetings.

Non-resident Ambassadors can also request courtesy and/or meetings as per section 1.5.2 through

a non-resident Head of Mission. The Note Verbale should include the flight details of when the

Transport will be booked and borne by the Ministry for Airport-Hotel-Airport transfers, and 
farewell calls organized by the Protocol Directorate.
1.8 Chargé d’Affaires a.i. 
If the sending State accredits a Head of Mission to Malta from another State it may establish a 
diplomatic mission headed by a Chargé d’Affaires in Malta.
A Chargé d’Affaires ad interim may also act provisionally as the Head of Mission. A Chargé 
d’Affaires ad interim shall be named if the post of a Head of Mission is vacant or a Head of Mission 
is for a reason unable to perform normal functions or is travelling out of Malta.
The name of the Chargé d’Affaires ad interim shall be notified to the Protocol Directorate by Note 
Verbale.
In situations where none of the members of the diplomatic staff is present, a member of the 
administrative or technical staff may be designated as the person in charge of the current affairs 
of the mission.

Immunities and Privileges of diplomatic and consular representatives, international organizations 
and representatives thereof in Malta are regulated by Chapter 191, Diplomatic Immunities and 
Privileges Act, (Act I of 1966), as amended by Acts XIV of 2000 and III of 2004, of the Laws of 
Malta, and by other subsidiary legislation and legal notices issued from time to time, together 
with established practices and conventions.
The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, 1961, provides for different categories of mission 
personnel. As a rule, these categories are: diplomatic agents, members of the administrative and 
technical staff, members of the service staff, private servants and locally engaged personnel.
The categories are defined primarily with reference to the functions performed. The immunities 
and privileges enjoyed by members of a mission differ considerably on the basis of the duties 
of the person. Should a member of a mission change from one category to another, the sending 
State should confirm such a change of responsibilities. 
In line with Article 11 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, the number of diplomats 
and administrative officers is to be commensurate to the size of the mission.
It should be noted that, as a rule, the Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs does not accept the 
assignment of Maltese nationals as diplomatic agents in Malta.
2.1  General Information  
Diplomatic missions must notify the Protocol Directorate as soon as possible of the appointment, 
arrival (and final departure) of members of diplomatic missions as well as members of their families 
and private servants (other than locally engaged personnel). 
International Organisations must also notify the Protocol Directorate as soon as possible of the 
appointment, arrival (and final departure) of all officials of the organisations as well as members 
of their families and private servants. 
The Protocol Directorate requests diplomatic missions to notify through a Note Verbale about 
locally engaged personnel taking up employment or leaving the mission.
2.2  Notifications
1 - Members of the Mission


<!-- page 13 -->
A diplomatic agent is the head of a mission or a member of the diplomatic staff of the mission. 
Diplomatic agents should in principle be of the nationality of the sending State. Diplomatic 
agents are expected to be in possession of a diplomatic passport, valid at least three years, to 
hold a recognized diplomatic rank, to perform diplomatic functions on a full-time basis and to be 
on normal rotation. 
Diplomatic agents and dependent family members forming part of their households enjoy 
diplomatic immunity.
Article 1 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations mentions the different categories of 
members of the staff of the diplomatic missions. The holders of the following ranks are considered 
to be members of the diplomatic staff as defined in Article 1(d) of the said Convention: 
 - 
Ambassador (Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary)/ High Commissioner/ Apostolic Nuncio
 - Minister Plenipotentiary,
 
 - Chargé d’Affaires ad interim,
 
 - Minister,
 
 - Minister Counsellor,
 
 - First Counsellor,
 
 - Second Counsellor,
 - Counsellor,
 
 - Commercial Counsellor,
 
 - First Secretary,
 
 - Second Secretary,
 
 - Third Secretary,
 - Attaché, including Defence Attaché (or Military, Naval or Air
 Attaché).
Article 2 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations also lists the main functions of 
diplomatic agents, as a rule, into six categories: 
 - representing the sending State;
 
 - protecting the sending State’s nationals within the receiving
 State; 
 - negotiating with the receiving State;
 
 - 
notifying the sending State of conditions and developments within the receiving State; 
 - promoting friendly relations between the two states;
 
 - developing economic, cultural, and scientific relations betwee
n the two states.
A member of the staff of the mission, who does not hold one of the diplomatic ranks listed above, 
will as a general rule be classified as a member of the administrative and technical staff of the 
mission or the service staff.

Members of the administrative and technical staff of the mission are expected to perform their 
duties at the mission on a full-time basis and to be in possession of an official (special or service) 
valid passport issued by the sending State. In principle, they should be nationals of the sending 
State.
Members of the administrative and technical staff of the mission and members of their families 
forming part of their households enjoy diplomatic immunity.
2.4  Administrative and Technical Staff   
Members of the service staff should be in the domestic service of the mission. They enjoy immunity 
only in respect of acts performed in the course of their duties. In principle, they should be nationals 
of the sending State in possession of a service passport. Family members of the service staff of 
the mission are not covered by the provisions of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations.
2.5  Service Staff   
Locally engaged personnel are either Maltese nationals or permanently resident in Malta at the 
moment of employment.
Missions may employ Maltese nationals or holders of a residence permit in Malta with the right 
to work as locally engaged personnel. Such locally engaged personnel may work in diplomatic 
missions and International Organisations as administrators or as service staff and as domestic 
service staff with the diplomatic staff. 
Their employment differs from the employment of those staff members who are assigned directly 
from the sending State. The latter are usually considered to be on a temporary assignment as 
administrative/technical staff or service staff, and are, as a rule, holders of an Official (special or 
service) passport.

Family members forming part of the household of a member of a mission or a consular officer 
enjoy the same privileges and immunities as the member of the mission or consular officer. 
According to the Maltese view, the following persons are included:
 - 
Wife/husband/registered partner or registered cohabitee;
 - Unmarried children under 21 years of age who reside exclusive
ly in the principal 
  
    
household;
 - Unmarried children who are attending an institution of higher
 education in Malta on a 
 
   
full-time basis but continue to form part of the household.
Children of diplomats up to the age of 21 years are considered family members, provided they live 
in the same household as the parents and are not married. Their status is identical to the status of 
the main beneficiary. Administrative personnel are treated equally.
Diplomatic Identity Cards are granted for children above the age limit of 21 on a case by case 
basis for example on the basis of a child undergoing studies and living in the same household or 
if a child has a medical condition which results in the child being a dependent. Immunities and 
privileges are the same as those of children up to the age of 21.
Where a member of the family forming part of the household of a diplomatic agent wishes 
to engage in employment in Malta, the Embassy of the sending State shall send a request, on 
behalf of that family member, for authorisation to engage in gainful occupation to the Protocol 
Directorate. Any such authorisation to engage in a gainful occupation in Malta will expire at the 
end of the assignment of the diplomatic agent concerned. A member of the family forming part 
of the household of a diplomatic agent shall not enjoy immunity from the civil and administrative 
jurisdiction of Malta with respect to any act carried out in the course of the gainful occupation 
of that family member. A family member will be subject to the tax and social security regime in 
Malta for all matters connected with their gainful occupation in Malta. The employment of family 
members of diplomatic agents from non-EU countries is to be governed by a bilateral agreement/ 
arrangement between the sending and the receiving State.

Private servant is defined as a person who is in the domestic service of a member of a mission. 
Private servants should be distinguished from members of the service staff who are in the 
domestic service of a mission.
Only diplomatic agents and their consular equivalents are accepted as employers of private 
servants. In principle, only one private servant for each household is allowed. Exceptions can be 
made for heads of missions for the employment of additional private servants. A needs assessment 
is made, taking into account the size of the employer’s household, access to subsidised childcare 
and special needs, etc.
Relatives of staff members will not be accepted as private servants.
The Protocol Directorate issues a Special Identity Card for a private servant after their arrival in 
Malta. However, a visa may be needed to enter Malta. A person who is granted a Special Identity 
Card by the Ministry as a private servant is exempt from the obligation to hold a work permit for 
their employment with a diplomatic agent or career consul.
Private servants who are third-country nationals and represent other nationalities than the 
sending State are required to apply for a visa according to visa regulations. The Mission is to 
inform the Ministry via Note Verbale of the arrival of the private servant and is to request the 
intervention of the Ministry to assist in facilitating the issuance of a visa. However, the decision 
to issue or refuse a visa is not at the discretion or competence of this Ministry. The Embassy is 
requested to attach a copy of the passport, the residential address of the private servant, the 
contract of employment, a job description and the person  responsible for their stay in Malta. 
When a visa is granted, the Ministry will inform the Mission. If the prospective private servant 
already resides in Malta then a copy of the residence permit is also to be provided.

The Diplomatic Corp in the Republic of Malta is formed by the Heads of Mission and the diplomatic 
agents accredited in Malta.
3.1  General Information  
The Dean of the Diplomatic Corps in Malta is the Apostolic Nuncio accredited to Malta and, as 
such further to representing the Diplomatic Corp accredited to Malta, he will always be given 
precedence before all other Heads of Mission.
The position of Acting Dean of the Diplomatic Corps in Malta is occupied by the longest serving 
resident Head of Mission, according to the date of the Presentation of the Letters of Credence to 
H.E. The President of Malta.
The list of order of precedence can be consulted on by visiting the website of the Ministry 
responsible for Foreign Affairs on https://foreign.gov.mt.
3.2  Dean of the Diplomatic Corps in Malta
The Ministry updates the list of the Diplomatic Corps accredited to Malta periodically. 
Every Mission and International Organisation must forward to the Protocol Directorate, by the 
last week of January of every year, the list by Order of Precedence of its accredited diplomatic 
agents, with indication of their civil status, and in such case (Head of Mission and Deputy Head of 
Mission) the name and surname of the spouse.
The list of the accredited diplomatic agents can be consulted by visiting the website of the 
Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs on https://foreign.gov.mt
3.3  Diplomatic Corps List
The Order of Precedence of the Diplomatic Corps in State functions and occasions is as follows: 
 1. 
 Dean of the Diplomatic Corps;
 2. 
 Heads of Mission according to the published list;
 3.
 Chargé d’Affaires a.i.;
 4.
 Deputy Heads of Mission;
 5.
 Other diplomatic agents according to the approved list by the Ministry.

In certain cases, a visa is required for legal entrance in Malta. The Ministry assists the following 
people in obtaining a visa to travel to Malta: foreign staff members accredited at diplomatic 
missions and international organisations based in Malta; family members forming part of privileged 
staff members’ households who wish to join them; and their private servants if applicable.
4.1  General Information  
Whether or not a visa is required depends on the following:
- Nationality
Nationals of most non-EU countries need a visa to enter Malta. A list of the countries whose 
nationals need a visa for a stay of up to a maximum of 90 days can be found on the below link:  
https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-01/Annex%201_en.pdf
(N.B. certain countries have different number of days allowed)
- Type of travel document
A person’s travel document determines whether or not a visa is needed, even if that person has 
more than one nationality. A person travelling on a passport of a country whose nationals require 
a visa must have a valid visa.

Diplomatic immunities are the most important prerogatives awarded by international law 
to diplomatic and consular missions and diplomatic agents accredited overseas. International 
obligations regarding immunities are intended to ensure that the persons granted immunity will 
not be impeded in an unnecessary way while carrying out their official duties and diplomatic 
relations.
When Embassies or foreign diplomatic staff are involved in legal disputes, the Protocol Directorate 
is called to moderate between a potential conflict of interest. On the one hand, Malta is required 
to observe international regulations regarding diplomatic immunities. On the other hand, it is 
in the interest of the foreign diplomatic community resident in Malta to respect the local legal 
system, as specified in article 41 of the 1961 Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, especially 
as regards the internal affairs of that State.
7.1  General Information  
Under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, the following premises and persons are 
inviolable:
 - A mission’s premises and its means of transport;
 - A mission’s archives and documents;
 - A mission’s official correspondence, the diplomatic bag and th
e diplomatic courier;
 - 
Diplomatic agents, members of the administrative and technical staff of the mission and  
 members of their families forming part of their households;
 - 
The private residences of diplomatic agents and members of the administrative and  
 technical staff of the mission, as well as their papers, correspondence and property.
  
 Property is only inviolable when used by persons enjoying privileges and immunities.
 - 
Diplomatic agents and members of their families, who pass through Malta, provided  
 they are travelling to or returning from a post.
7.2  Diplomatic Immunity
1 - Diplomatic Immunities and Privileges


<!-- page 52 -->
The premises of the mission are the buildings or parts of buildings and the land ancillary thereto, 
irrespective of ownership, used for the purposes of the mission including the official residence of 
the Head of Mission. The premises of the mission are inviolable and cannot be violated without the 
consent of the Head of Mission. Accordingly, the premises of the mission, furnishings and other 
property thereon as well as means of transport are immune from search, requisition, attachment 
or execution.
Maltese authorities take all appropriate steps to protect the premises of the mission against any 
intrusion or damage and to prevent any disturbance of the peace of the mission or impairment 
of its dignity.
7.3   Inviolability of the Mission Premises,  
  
Residences and other Property
The person of a diplomatic agent is inviolable and is not liable to any form of arrest or detention. 
Malta is under a special duty to take all appropriate measures to protect the inviolability of a 
diplomatic agent and to prevent any attack on the person, freedom or dignity.
The residence of a diplomatic agent and of a member of the administrative and technical staff 
enjoys the same inviolability and protection as the premises of a mission. Inviolability applies also 
to their other property, such as motor vehicles.
The personal baggage of a diplomatic agent is exempt from inspection unless there are serious 
grounds for presuming that it contains articles that cannot legally be exported or imported. 
The inspection will be conducted in the presence of the diplomatic agent or a representative. 
However, subject to the relevant regulations on aviation safety the person and the personal 
baggage are liable to screening for security purposes in the airports. Refusal of the screening and 
further inspection may lead to a prohibition to board the flight. 
7.4  Diplomatic Immunity
2 - Diplomatic Immunities and Privileges


<!-- page 53 -->
Article 41 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations establishes that it is the duty of all 
persons enjoying privileges and immunities to respect the laws and regulations of the receiving 
State. While mission staff may enjoy criminal immunity, the Protocol Directorate will not accept 
criminal acts committed by mission staff or their family members. It is in the interest of all 
concerned to avoid breaches of the law. 
7.5  Respect for Local Laws and Regulations
Persons with diplomatic immunity must respect traffic rules and laws of Malta.
Most infringements of local law concern road and traffic regulations. Illegal parking and over 
speeding carries a fine. The owner of the vehicle is responsible for the vehicle and has primary 
responsibility to pay the parking or other fines. No immunity can be invoked to the Ministry or any 
other Maltese authority on parking fines or over speeding.
The Passing of Red lights is set at a fine (multa) of €100, speeding is set at €34.94 for speeds 
below 15km/hr over the speed limit and €69.88 for speeds above 15km/hr over the speed limit, 
parking in an illegal place is at €23.29 unless it is an obstruction in which case it would be €104.82.  
Driving on the wrong side incurrs a penalty of up to €1,200. Fines (multa) can be revised by the 
competent authorities from time to time.
Points to be deducted are as per schedule found at the sixth schedule 4. 
7.5.1 Traffic Violations
Every driver is obliged to pull over if requested to do so by the police, local wardens or any other 
traffic authority, and to present the registration (log book) of the vehicle and the diplomatic 
identity card issued by the Ministry. The traffic authorities will return the documents after 
verification.
7.5.2 Traffic Control

Malta. The Ministry requests persons enjoying diplomatic immunity to respect Maltese Law and

misbehaviour and will take action when a violation of the law is brought to its attention by the

In this Chapter, privileged persons means Heads of Mission, diplomatic agents and members of 
the administrative and technical staff under the terms of the Diplomatic Immunities and Privileges 
Act of 1966. No exemption from taxes or any other duties are accorded to any person who is a 
citizen or permanent resident in Malta.
Service staff, domestic staff, honorary consuls and members of the locally engaged personnel are 
not entitled to any exemption on motor vehicles.
There is no fixed limit to the number of vehicles a mission may possess for official use, but the 
number may not exceed the number of members of the diplomatic agents and members of the 
administrative and technical staff of the mission. As a general rule, a quota is applied for every 
mission. It is strongly recommended that a mission seeks agreement with the Protocol Directorate 
prior to purchasing a new vehicle. The Directorate will not issue CD plates and grant the related 
exemptions if there is no prior authorisation.
For International Organisations, the rules on acquisition, possession and transfer of motor vehicles 
are contained in the relevant headquarters agreement. 
Accredited diplomatic agents are only entitled to import or purchase one motor vehicle for their 
personal use and one motor vehicle for the spouse subject to being a holder of a valid diplomatic 
identity card, driving licence and must be residing in Malta.
Administrative and technical staff members are permitted to import or purchase one motor 
vehicle per family.
On termination of a tour of duty of a privileged person, the mission/organization is to inform the 
Protocol Directorate with immediate effect, through a Note Verbale, whether the vehicle is to

Directorate within Transport Malta.
20.1  General Information
The Protocol Directorate processes requests concerning over flights and/or landings over the 
Maltese territory.
State aircraft means any aircraft used for military, customs and police (in accordance with EC 
Regulation 1265/07 and ICAO Article 3b of the Chicago Convention).
Requests for diplomatic clearance also apply to aircraft:
 - 
owned or operated by a State and used, for the time being, only on government non-  
 commercial service (reference Article 236 of the UN Convention of the Law of the Sea);
 - 
carrying Heads of State, Heads of Government, Government Ministers and those afforded  
 VIP status by the Government of Malta;
 - conducting activities on behalf of the UN.
All 
over flights, and/or landings, over the Maltese territory (12 nautical miles) by a State aircraft 
require previous authorisation by the Ministry.
Applications for authorisation are to be made via a Note Verbale enclosing the 
Diplomatic Clearance form and are to be sent electronically on e-mail address:  
diplo-clearances.foreign@gov.mt

The Maltese 
Government
21

<!-- page 180 -->
The system of Government in Malta is a parliamentary representative democratic republic, 
whereby The President of Malta is the constitutional Head of State. Executive Authority is vested 
in the President of the Republic, with the general direction and control of the Government of 
Malta remaining with the Prime Minister of Malta who is the head of government and the cabinet 
of ministers. Legislative power is vested in the Parliament of Malta which consists of the President 
of the Republic and the unicameral House of Representatives of Malta with the Speaker presiding 
officer of the legislative body. Judicial power remains with the Chief Justice and the Judiciary of 
Malta. 
The Maltese Constitution was adopted on 21 September 1964 and has been the country’s legal 
order ever since. Any law or action in violation of the Constitution is considered null and void, and 
there is a strict three-tier entrenchment basis that must be gone through for any amendments 
to take place. 
The Constitution covers topics across the board, including Citizenship, Fundamental Rights and 
Freedoms of the Individual, the Judiciary, the Public Service, Local Councils and more.
It safeguards the fundamental human rights of citizens, and forces a separation between the 
executive, judicial and legislative powers, with regular elections based on universal suffrage.
For more information on the Government of Malta: www.gov.mt
21.1  General Information
Upon taking up the position, the President takes an oath to ‘preserve, protect and defend’ 
the Constitution. Additionally, as part of the substantial and wide-reaching role, the President 
promulgates laws, receives foreign ambassadors, and may dissolve the House of Representatives 
on the advice of the Prime Minister or following the passage of a no confidence motion in the 
Government. The term of office of the President is of five (5) years.
For more information on the Office of the President: www.president.gov.mt
21.2  The President
1 - The Maltese Government


<!-- page 181 -->
The Prime Minister is the Head of Government, as well as a Member of Parliament. He also advises 
the President on the appointment of the other Ministers.
Constitutionally, the Prime Minister is responsible for engaging Permanent Secretaries as well 
as giving advice to the President on the appointment of members to the Judiciary and other 
constitutional bodies.
Supported by the Office of the Prime Minister, the Prime Minister also provides leadership and 
direction for a stable and effective Government.
For more information on the Office of the Prime Minister: www.opm.gov.mt
21.3  The Prime Minister
The Maltese Parliament comprises the President of the Republic, Mr Speaker, the Deputy Speaker, 
and the Members of Parliament.
Through its elected representatives, the House of Representatives is accountable to the Maltese 
public for the provision and conduct of the representative government in the interest of Maltese 
citizens.
In line with this, the Office of the Clerk, the legislature’s secretariat is duty-bound to deliver 
effective, apolitical, professional and innovative services to support the efficient conduct of the 
House of Representatives, its committees as well as a range of services and facilities for Members 
of Parliament.
Members of Parliament are elected every five (5) years, or prior if the President on the advice of 
the Prime Minister dissolves Parliament and fixes the date for a General Election. The electoral 
system used is of proportional representation by means of the Single Transferrable Vote. All 
citizens above the age of 16 and whose name is published in the latest Electoral Register before 
the General Elections is held are eligible to vote.
For more information on the House of Representatives: www.parlament.mt

The Maltese Judiciary is made up of serving Judges and Magistrates who have been appointed to 
sit in the Superior and Inferior Courts. The courts in Malta are divided into Superior and Inferior 
courts.
Judges sit on the Superior Courts, which, in Malta, are made up of the Constitutional Court, the 
Court of Appeal, the Court of Criminal Appeal, the Criminal Court and the Civil Court. The Inferior 
Courts are the Court of Magistrates (Malta) and the Court of Magistrates (Gozo). The latter court 
has a both superior and an inferior jurisdiction.
The Maltese judicial system is a two-tier system comprising: a court of first instance, presided 
over by a judge or magistrate, and a court of appeal.
There are also various tribunals that deal with specific areas of the law and have varying degrees 
of competence. Decisions pronounced by these Tribunals are almost all dealt with by the Court 
of Appeal.
For more information on the Judiciary: www.judiciarymalta.gov.mt
21.5  The Judiciary
Local Government was established in 1993 following the Maltese Parliament’s approval of the Local 
Councils Act (Chapter 363 of the Laws of Malta) on 30 June 1993. This law set up Local Councils 
in 67 localities (later increased to 68). Furthermore, it now serves as a regulatory mechanism for 
Local Councils’ operations. Today, Malta has 68 Local Councils – 54 in Malta and 14 in Gozo. 
For more information on Local Government: www.localgovernment.gov.mt

## Main findings

- N-Lex explicitly says Cyprus cannot currently be searched there. Its CyLaw link is a discovery lead only. The official Government Printing Office Gazette is the primary publication route; its English interface says information is available in Greek only.
- Gov.cy Migration Department pages separate EU/EEA citizens, third-country family members, family of Cypriot citizens, extended family, and Withdrawal Agreement routes. Forms are often linked from changing parent pages as DOCX or PDF attachments. The category names, sponsor nationality, and Swiss/EEA scope must remain explicit.
- The Law Commissioner publishes selected English translations and consolidations, but they have stated cut-off dates and are not authentic texts. Verify amendment chains against the Greek Gazette.
- Cyprus visa and Schengen information needs an inconsistency check: an MFA hub statement about some family-member residence cards conflicts with a January 2026 Government PDF about Cyprus-issued residence documents and Schengen travel. Do not encode either statement as an operative rule until the card type, sponsor, and current EU law are reconciled.
- The Government Gazette is a Greek-only legacy dynamic route, and many newer gov.cy pages returned 403 to the research client. That is a client-side verification limitation, not evidence that the public pages are unavailable. No documented API or registration route was identified in the bounded search.
- Remedies, access-to-information, privacy, legal aid, and court sources are maintained by separate authorities. Public blank forms and guidance may be reviewed; personal submissions, case files, and identity data must not be collected.

All 43 entries remain candidates. There are zero accepted sources and no Cyprus runtime rule verdict.

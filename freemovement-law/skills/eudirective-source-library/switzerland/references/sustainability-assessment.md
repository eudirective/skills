## Currentness and scope signals

- The Switzerland–EU Bilaterals III package includes an amending protocol to the AFMP. It was signed on 2 March 2026 and submitted to Parliament on 13 March 2026. The official government status page says Parliament and potentially voters must still approve the package. Treat this as a pending amendment, not operative law; monitor parliamentary, referendum, enactment and commencement events. [Package documents and status](https://www.europa.eda.admin.ch/en/dispatch-swiss-eu-package), [Federal Council status page](https://www.admin.ch/en/swiss-eu-relations).
- SEM's current third-country family procedure instruction is a useful central process source, but the legal responsibility spans SEM, Swiss representations and cantonal migration authorities. The authority/contact directory is only a starting point for canton-level work.
- SEM's current visa FAQ describes a 30-day written objection to a visa refusal and an advance on costs. Keep this route separate from cantonal appeals against residence decisions and any later Federal Administrative Court proceeding.
- SEM's AFMP family-reunification factsheet dates from 2024. It needs a currentness comparison with SEM's 2026 directives and the pending protocol before it is used as the primary route record.
- Swiss family cases require distinct sponsor categories: Swiss citizens, EU/EFTA citizens, third-country residents, recognised refugees/provisionally admitted persons, and people with acquired UK rights. A general family-reunification index does not establish coverage of each route.

## Proposed implementation priorities

1. Extend the Fedlex rule from the FADP to the remaining identified laws and treaties. Resolve each SR number to the in-force ELI record, select and preserve a dated manifestation, validate original-language identity, and independently establish whether a later version exists. The current capture is a 2023 consolidation and must not be presumed current.
2. Reconcile the authoritative Official Compilation publication with the Classified Compilation/current consolidated view. Do not treat an English translation or current-looking consolidated page as the authoritative text without checking Fedlex's legal status and the enactment's publication mode.
3. Build separate adapters or source groups for legislation, SEM dated instruction PDFs, visa lists, mission pages, cantonal pages, and court decisions. Give each group its own identity markers, date capture, change detection, and conversion review.
4. Turn the initial SEM page/PDF captures into parent-index discovery and replacement checks. Require title, instruction number, date, language, and attachment identity for PDFs; preserve source bytes and review complex tables and country annexes visually. Replace the external one-off PDF extraction with a reproducible local converter before claiming replayable conversion.
5. Track Bilaterals III as a legislative lifecycle, including parliamentary decisions, referendum status, implementing federal acts, Official Compilation publication and commencement. Do not activate future rules from a signed agreement alone.
6. Expand cantonal and mission coverage from official directories. Identify local law, applications, document requirements, procedure, fees, appeals and privacy oversight by canton/post; treat Zurich and Istanbul only as initial samples.
7. Define bounded court searches using official identifiers and language variants, record the court's publication limits, and confirm terms, paging, export, and stable decision links before automation.
8. Keep data-protection and complaint forms informational. Do not submit forms, authenticate, schedule appointments, or capture personal applicant data during source monitoring.

## Scope boundary

The discovery review covered central federal publishers, Istanbul as one mission sample, and Zurich as one canton sample. German/French/English materials were encountered; Italian and Romansh coverage is incomplete. Capture rules have been exercised on one Fedlex manifestation and 27 direct official page/PDF routes, but discovery recurrence, broad legal version resolution, and full canton/mission coverage are unfinished. No source content was accepted, and no conclusion is made about the complete Swiss legal-source universe or the merits of any individual family route.

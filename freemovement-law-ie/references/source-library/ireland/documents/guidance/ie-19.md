# Ireland: joining an EEA or Swiss national

**Source ID:** ie-19  
**Publisher:** Immigration Service Delivery (ISD), Department of Justice, Home Affairs and Migration  
**Official page:** <https://www.irishimmigration.ie/coming-to-join-family-in-ireland/joining-an-eea-or-swiss-national/>  
**Checked:** 29 October 2026  
**Language:** English

## Route summary

This ISD page addresses family members seeking to rely on Directive 2004/38/EC through an EU/EEA/Swiss citizen exercising free-movement rights in Ireland. It provides a Directive checklist and directs those who do not meet its criteria to the ordinary visa process.

The page distinguishes short stays from residence beyond three months. It describes a short-stay C visa route for visa-required non-EEA family members who accompany or join a qualifying sponsor. Holders of specified residence cards of a family member of a Union citizen can travel visa-free for up to 90 days, subject to carrying and producing the card. For a stay beyond three months, the page says an Irish residence card must be obtained; another Member State’s card does not itself authorize a long-term Irish stay.

The page separates a family member joining an Irish citizen residing in Ireland: Directive 2004/38 does not apply to that route, and national rules apply. It includes family categories, fee treatment and supporting-document guidance, including evidence of identity, family link, dependency or household membership, sponsor free-movement activity, and translations/authentication.

## Limits and maintenance

The live ISD page was opened on 29 October 2026. This is official operational guidance, not a substitute for Directive 2004/38, Irish implementing law or an individual decision. EEA, Swiss, EU and Irish-national rules have distinct legal bases. The page’s checklist and fees can change; verify them and the sponsor’s route before use.

Recheck the Directive checklist, visa type, document, fee and appeal links at each review.

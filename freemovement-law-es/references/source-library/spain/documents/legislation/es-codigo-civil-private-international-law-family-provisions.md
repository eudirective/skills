## Scope

This maintained note maps the family-related conflict-of-laws provisions in the official consolidated Código Civil. It is a scoped guide to the publisher's text, not a replacement for the full Code or an English translation.

The BOE page identifies the 1889 Royal Decree publishing the Civil Code and offers a consolidated informational text. ## Relevant provisions

- **Article 9 (personal law and family relationships):** nationality determines an individual's personal law for capacity, civil status, family rights and duties, and succession. Article 9(2) sets connecting factors for marital effects, including common personal law at marriage, a permitted pre-marriage choice, common habitual residence immediately after marriage, and finally the place of celebration. Article 9(3) addresses the law validating matrimonial-property agreements. Article 9(4) begins the rules for determining and characterizing natural filiation.
- **Article 10:** contains additional choice-of-law rules, including rules concerning property. Its full scope is broader than family status; consult the article directly when property questions arise.
- **Article 11 (form):** addresses the formal validity of acts and contracts, including acts made abroad and acts authorized by Spanish diplomatic or consular officials.
- **Article 12 (general conflict rules):** addresses characterization under Spanish law, remission to foreign law, public policy, evasion of mandatory Spanish law, states with multiple legal systems, and ex officio application of Spanish conflict rules. The BOE page displays its latest update as published 8 January 2000, effective 8 January 2001.
- **Article 16 (interregional conflicts):** adapts the Chapter IV rules to conflicts among civil-law systems within Spain; personal law is determined by civil vecindad, subject to the article's stated qualifications.
- **Article 107 (marital status):** marriage nullity and its effects follow the law governing the marriage's celebration; legal separation and divorce follow EU or Spanish private international law rules. The BOE displays the latest update as 3 July 2015, effective 23 July 2015.
- **Article 108 (filiation):** distinguishes natural and adoptive filiation and states that marital, non-marital, and adoptive filiation have the same effects under the Code. The BOE displays the latest update as 1 March 2023, effective 2 March 2023.

The article-level version dates differ. The BOE page's overall consolidation date must not be substituted for the specific update history shown under each provision.

## Source record

The official Migration Department page describes the card-shaped Lithuanian certificates issued to EU citizens who have temporary or permanent residence rights. It says the certificates record biometric data for identity verification, including a facial image and two fingerprints, and that the newer certificate format began to be issued on 8 November 2022.

The page links four images named as the front and reverse of the temporary certificate (`AT_2022_aversas`, `AT_2022_reversas`) and the permanent certificate (`AR_2022_aversas`, `AR_2022_reversas`). This record preserves the page's image references; it does not independently authenticate a specimen or describe its visible security features.

The page also links to the Migration Department's Migris site for more information. The linked application service was not used to establish the content of this page.

## Currentness and legal dependency

The page cites Regulation (EU) 2019/1157 as the format basis for the 2022 certificates. EUR-Lex records that Regulation as no longer in force from 9 July 2025, declared invalid in Case C-61/22 and replaced by Council Regulation (EU) 2025/1208, which applies from 10 July 2025. The 2022 page's citation is therefore historical and must not be treated as the current EU legal basis.

The webpage remains useful as dated evidence about the Lithuanian document format announced in 2022 and the Migration Department's linked specimen images. Current eligibility, biometric requirements, issuance procedure, document validity, and EU-law requirements must be checked against the current Lithuanian legislation and Regulation (EU) 2025/1208.

## Publisher wording to preserve as an issue

The English page describes the “Certificate of the Right to Permanent Residence” as granting a right to “temporarily reside” for the specified period. That conflicts with the certificate's own permanent-residence label and appears to be a drafting or translation error. This maintained record flags the wording without silently correcting it. Do not rely on that sentence to determine legal status or duration.

The page's body says EU citizens are the subjects of the certificate system, while its two image captions describe the temporary and permanent certificates for citizens of EU and EFTA member states. That is the page's wording; it does not itself establish that EU and EFTA rights, procedures, or eligibility conditions are identical.

## Disposition and limits

Accepted as a dated official document-identification reference and maintained English summary, not as a current statement of governing EU law or a complete residence procedure. Its legal citation is stale, and the permanent-certificate description contains an apparent error. The page does not set out a complete application checklist, processing time, fee, appeal route, or full eligibility test.

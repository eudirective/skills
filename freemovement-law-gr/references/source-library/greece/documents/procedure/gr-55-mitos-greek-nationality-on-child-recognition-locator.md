## Procedure text

- **Fee:** No fee for the procedure. A consular fee may apply to an application submitted at a Greek consular authority under Presidential Decree 16/2014.
- **Estimated completion time:** 18 months.
- **Competent body:** Ministry of Interior.
- **Output:** Decision.
- **Registry updated:** Integrated Information System for Nationality (Ολοκληρωμένο Πληροφοριακό Σύστημα Ιθαγένειας).

### Description and scope

This procedure concerns a foreign national born out of wedlock and legally recognised as the child of a Greek father. Greek nationality is granted if the child is a minor at the time of recognition.

The date nationality is acquired is the date the notarial recognition act is drawn up or the date the judicial recognition act becomes non-appealable.

If recognition occurs after the child reaches adulthood, MITOS points to the naturalisation procedure for expatriates: https://www.ypes.gr/g-g-ithageneias/diadikasies-ktisis-e-i/politografisi-omogenon.

### Application

The application is titled “Application for determination of nationality due to recognition by a Greek father.” It may be submitted in person or by post.

The father and/or mother may apply while the child is a minor. The child may apply after reaching adulthood if recognition took place while they were a minor. If the application is submitted to a Greek consular authority abroad, a consular fee may apply under Presidential Decree 16/2014 on Consular Fees and Royalties.

The page lists an identification document as the means of authentication, identification and signature.

### Eligibility requirements

1. The person must be the child of a foreign mother and a Greek father, born out of wedlock; there must be no presumption of birth in wedlock under Greek Civil Code Article 1465.
2. The person must not already have acquired Greek nationality by another lawful means before recognition.
3. The child must be under 18 on the date they are recognised as the child of a Greek father.
4. The recognising father must be a Greek citizen when recognition takes place.
5. The recognition act must be valid under Greek law, determined under Greek Civil Code Articles 11 and 20.

### Supporting documents

MITOS displays the following entries. Some are alternatives to one another, as identified in the page.

1. **Police-issued identity card.** Required if the application is filed by the Greek father. The page also indicates an ex-officio digital copy through interoperability.
2. **Child’s birth certificate issued in Greece.** Required when the child was born in Greece; it must be issued by the competent Greek authorities.
3. **Foreign birth certificate.** For a child born outside Greece, provide the competent foreign authority’s certificate, with Apostille or the corresponding consular certification where the issuing country has not ratified the Hague Convention, and an official translation. The certificate may function as the recognition act; in that case, the competent consular authority draws up a recognition report. This is an alternative to document 2.
4. **Voluntary notarial recognition act.** The Greek father recognises the child with the foreign mother’s consent. The deed must comply with Greek Civil Code Article 1475. If recognition occurs after the child turns three, the foreign mother must hold a lawful residence permit for the deed to be drawn up.
5. **Court judgment recognising paternity.** For a Greek judgment, provide a copy and a certificate that it is non-appealable. If recognition rests on an irrevocable foreign judgment, its res judicata must also be recognised in Greece. This is an alternative to document 4.
6. **Greek father’s municipal-registration certificate.** It must show registration in the competent municipal roll and establish that the father held Greek nationality when recognition took place.
7. **Foreign mother’s family-status certificate.** Provide the original with Apostille or corresponding consular certification, plus an official translation. It is used to establish that the child is the child of an unmarried mother or that the presumption that a child born in wedlock is from that marriage has been rebutted.
8. **Foreign mother’s certificate of non-impediment.** Provide the original with Apostille or corresponding consular certification, plus an official translation. This is an alternative to document 7.
9. **Foreign mother’s solemn declaration of non-impediment.** May be submitted if it is objectively impossible to issue a certificate of non-impediment. If not in Greek, it needs an official translation. It must be accompanied by a duly certified and officially translated certificate from the competent foreign authorities confirming that a certificate of non-impediment or similar certificate is not issued. This is an alternative to document 7.
10. **Exact copy of the foreign mother’s valid passport.** Used to confirm her particulars.
11. **Other proof of identity of the foreign mother.** This is an alternative to document 10.
12. **Foreign mother’s residence permit.** Required if the application is also submitted by the foreign mother to the competent Greek nationality service.

The procedure record shows that the identity card and the mother’s residence permit are administrative requirements. The document count and alternative-document relationships follow the official page’s structured fields.

### Fees and redress

The procedure is shown as free. A consular fee may apply for filing through a Greek consular authority abroad under Presidential Decree 16/2014.

A special administrative appeal may be lodged with the Minister of Interior against decisions of Regional Directors of Nationality and decisions issued “pursuant to authorisation,” under Article 8 of Law 3200/1955 and Article 45 of Law 4604/2019, as amended by Article 37 of Law 4674/2020. The appeal must include a EUR 100 fee receipt. The page records tacit rejection for lack of reply.

### Procedure steps

1. **Application and reference number.** File with the Regional Directorate of Nationality for the parents’ place of residence in Greece, or with a Greek consular authority if they live abroad. A minor’s application is filed by the father and/or mother; an adult whose recognition took place while a minor may apply personally. The application receives a file number and is assigned to the competent department. A consular filing is sent in a diplomatic bag to the Directorate responsible for the municipality where the Greek father is registered. An adult may contact a nationality service for the municipality where they wish to register.
2. **Assignment to rapporteur.** The competent head assigns the file to a rapporteur through the Nationality Integrated Information System.
3. **Check of requirements.** The rapporteur checks the file’s completeness, supporting-document legality and whether the requirements for a nationality decision are met.
4. **Search for legal information or applicable law.** If the Directorate-General for Nationality lacks legal information for the country where recognition occurred, it corresponds with the competent Greek consulate to check whether recognition was lawful under that country’s law and whether it fully assimilates the child to a child born in wedlock, without a condition, time limit or revocation, as required for legal effects under Greek law.
5. **Search for additional supporting documents.** If submitted documents are incomplete or not in the required form, the authority requests compliant documents.
6. **Preparation of draft decision.** A draft accepting or rejecting the application is prepared.
7. **Signing of decision.** The rapporteur, head of unit and director sign the draft.
8. **Issuance of nationality decision.** The decision determining Greek nationality due to recognition is issued. The page provides no separate narrative description for this step.
9. **Dispatch to National Printing House.** A summary of the decision is sent electronically for publication in the Government Gazette.
10. **Publication in the Government Gazette.** The National Printing House publishes a summary of the decision.
11. **Upload to DIAVGEIA.** After Gazette publication, the decision summary is also uploaded to the DIAVGEIA programme website.
12. **Notice and municipal-registration order.** A document is drawn up and sent to the competent Greek municipality ordering the recognised child’s registration in the municipal roll.
13. **Rejection decision.** If requirements are not met, the competent director issues a rejection decision.
14. **Notice of rejection.** A copy of the rejection decision is sent to the applicant and all stakeholders.

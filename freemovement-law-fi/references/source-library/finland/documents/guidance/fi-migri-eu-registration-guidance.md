## Who the page addresses

Migri directs citizens of an EU Member State, Liechtenstein or Switzerland to apply for EU registration when they will stay in Finland for over three months at a time. Nordic citizens (Denmark, Iceland, Norway and Sweden) use the Digital and Population Data Services Agency route to register personal data and do not apply to Migri for EU registration.

Migri says registration is valid until further notice and need not be extended. The page states a right to permanent residence after five years of continuous lawful residence in Finland. Those statements describe the agency's public guidance; check the current statute and live page for an individual case.

## Requirements described

The indexed page lists these requirements:

1. Citizenship of an EU Member State, Liechtenstein or Switzerland.
2. A valid passport or identity card.
3. A qualifying residence ground: employment, self-employment, study, or family membership. It also describes sufficient resources as a possible ground.
4. Sufficient resources for the applicant and family where applicable. Migri says there is no fixed amount for a resources-based route and assesses personal circumstances; resource evidence requirements differ by residence ground.
5. No danger to public order, security or public health.

For employees and self-employed people, the page says no fixed amount of money is required, but the application needs evidence of the work or business. Students must provide an assurance of sufficient resources and evidence that studies have started. Migri says a change in the person's residence ground does not by itself require a new registration.

## Special route for a family member of a Finnish citizen

The page treats this as a cross-border free-movement route. It says the Finnish citizen must have exercised free movement by living for at least three months in another EU Member State or a comparable country. It also says:

- the family relationship must have begun or strengthened while the family lived in another EU Member State;
- the Finnish citizen must move to Finland before the applicant if they do not move at the same time;
- both must move directly from that other Member State to Finland; and
- a Finnish citizen who holds another EU nationality but has lived only in Member States where they are a citizen has not exercised free movement for this purpose.

Migri says sufficient-resource documents are not required for the family-member-of-a-Finnish-citizen registration route it describes. Check the exact current legal basis and the separate residence-card route before applying this guidance to a particular family.

## Supporting documents listed by category

The page gives category-specific examples rather than one universal attachment list:

- **Employee:** employment contract or employer proof of a valid employment relationship; some zero-hours applicants may add recent payslips and bank statements.
- **Self-employed:** company-register extract, financial statements, tax decisions, client contracts or invoices, YEL insurance and recent bank statements. For light entrepreneurship through an invoicing service, it lists the commission agreement, work invoices and account evidence.
- **Unemployed or unable to work:** evidence that employment or self-employment ended or that work capacity changed, such as an employer statement, unemployment certificate, training evidence or a recent medical certificate.
- **Student:** proof of study and assurance of sufficient resources. Migri says an admission letter alone is insufficient; the study must have started.
- **Spouse:** proof of family ties. The page describes legalisation for relevant non-Nordic and non-EU documents and exceptions where the marriage is registered in Finland or at DVV.
- **Cohabiting partner:** evidence of at least two years' cohabitation or a child in joint custody.
- **Parent or guardian, child, dependent parent or other relative:** proof of family ties, custody where relevant, and evidence of dependency for dependent relatives.
- **Resources-based application:** evidence such as regular income, property, bank statements, or sponsor evidence showing that support is genuinely at the applicant's disposal.

The page says originals must be presented at the service appointment. Documents not in Finnish, Swedish or English generally need an authorised translation into one of those languages. It identifies an exception for specified EU-issued civil-status documents accompanied by a multilingual standard form. Check live instructions for the exact documents and translation rules.

## Application and decision flow

The indexed page sets out this route:

1. Apply from inside Finland through Enter Finland, add supporting documents, or use the paper form. Migri says the application must be submitted by the applicant, except that a parent may apply for a child.
2. Pay the processing fee through the stated online or service-point route.
3. Attend a Migri service point to prove identity, bringing the valid identity document and original attachments.
4. Wait for the decision; Migri may request more information. For work- or study-based applications, the relevant work or studies must have started before registration can be granted.
5. Supplement the application if needed through Enter Finland or the stated paper route.
6. Receive the registration certificate at the service point, in Enter Finland or by post, depending on the decision route and whether evidence is complete.
7. The page states that a person dissatisfied with the decision has a statutory right to appeal to an administrative court; decision-specific appeal instructions control.

The page also distinguishes EU registration from obtaining a Finnish identity card or registering a municipality of residence: it directs those follow-up processes to the police and DVV respectively.

## Limits and maintenance

- This is agency guidance, not legislation. The Finnish Aliens Act, Directive 2004/38 and relevant case law control legal interpretation.
- The page covers EU, Liechtenstein and Swiss registration; the Nordic registration route is distinct.
- The family-member-of-a-Finnish-citizen rule depends on genuine exercise of free movement and is not a general domestic family route.
- Direct retrieval failed, so the maintained text summary is based on the official indexed rendering, not a byte-for-byte or full HTML capture.
- Recheck the live page, forms, fees, documents, translation instructions, and appeal information before use.

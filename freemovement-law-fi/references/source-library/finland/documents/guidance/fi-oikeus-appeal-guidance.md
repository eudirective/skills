## Decision notice and first remedy

The page directs readers to the decision itself for the time limit and the authority that receives a request for rectification or an appeal. It says an interested party may appeal an authority decision, including a decision that a case is inadmissible, to an administrative court. In some matters the person must first seek rectification from the authority that made the decision.

For this purpose, the page describes an interested party as a person or organization to whom the decision is addressed or who is directly affected. In some municipal matters, the affected group may include all residents of the municipality.

## Administrative-court procedure

An appeal to an administrative court must be in writing. The page says the decision's appeal instructions provide more detail on drafting and filing. Administrative courts generally decide cases in writing; an oral hearing may take place at a party's request or where the court considers one necessary. If a hearing is held, the parties, the deciding authority, and where appropriate other persons are summoned.

Where an initial rectification request did not change the decision, the page says an administrative-court appeal may also be filed.

## Enforcement while a challenge is pending

As a general description, the page says an authority usually enforces its decision only after the appeal period expires or an appeal has been resolved. It identifies exceptions, including taxes and other public debts that remain payable during appeal, and municipal decisions that may be enforced while an appeal is pending.

An appellant may ask the administrative court to prohibit or suspend enforcement. The court may also do so on its own initiative.

## Further and extraordinary appeals

An administrative-court decision may be appealed to the Supreme Administrative Court (KHO) where KHO grants leave to appeal.

The page says that in some cases KHO may annul a final administrative-court decision or restore a missed time limit. Its examples include substantial new evidence arising after judgment that could materially affect the case and was unavailable for reasons beyond the appellant's control, or a manifest error of law or other error capable of materially affecting the decision.

## Authority-specific examples listed

The page links to separate appeal channels for traffic penalties and fine orders, competition/procurement matters, accident matters, legal-aid decisions, enforcement decisions, and decisions of the Prison and Probation Service.

For traffic matters, it describes a request for administrative review to the police or other authority that issued the traffic penalty fee. A fixed penalty fine, penal order, or fine order may be appealed to the district court for the territory where the fine was imposed. It lists a 30-day period from notification for fixed penalty fines and fine orders; for penal orders, it lists 60 days from consent or 30 days from the order date.

These examples concern those specific decision types and should not be generalized to immigration decisions.

## Source identity and limits

Oikeus.fi describes itself as a public legal-information portal maintained by the Ministry of Justice and providing basic legal information and links to judicial bodies and services. This is broad public guidance, not an immigration-specific procedure code. It does not supply a universal deadline, recipient, form, effect of appeal, or right to suspend enforcement for every authority decision. The operative decision notice and governing statute determine the route, deadline, and competent court in a particular case.

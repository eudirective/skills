# Malta bilingual verification

Use the language-pair register at references/source-library/malta/references/language-pairs.json and the source-acceptance record together. A language-pair entry records discovery and version metadata; it does not certify legal equivalence.

1. Identify the source as Maltese legislation, subsidiary legislation, judgment, guidance, or EU law.
2. For national statutes, locate the official English and Maltese representation under the same ELI identity. Confirm the returned text itself is in the claimed language; a URL suffix, selector or portal label alone is not enough.
3. Compare publication dates, point-in-time editions, amendments, commencement and instrument identifiers before comparing wording.
4. Align the actual provisions, definitions and numbering; do not infer matching content from matching article numbers.
5. When material, compare obligations, negations, exceptions, conditions, deadlines, powers, remedies and legal effects in both texts.
6. Apply Constitution Article 74's Maltese-text precedence only to national laws and after checking for a specific statutory exception. Do not apply it automatically to EU instruments or judgments. For court-language issues, check Constitution Article 5, Code of Organization and Civil Procedure Chapter 12 Article 21, Chapter 189 and the actual language of the judicial record.
7. Report which official versions were consulted, edition alignment, comparison status, any material difference, applicable language rule, and the exact unresolved gap.

Keep the official English reference even when Maltese prevails in a statutory conflict. Do not describe an unofficial or machine-generated English rendering as an official version. The source pack currently accepts official links only and no local national-law copies; do not package restricted statutory text. Keep Maltese-language EU texts separate from Malta national law.

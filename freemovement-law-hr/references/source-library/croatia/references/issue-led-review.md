The accepted Free Legal Aid Act is relevant to access to remedies, but does not establish blanket legal-aid eligibility in immigration cases. Article 5 enumerates eligible foreign-national categories; Article 13(2)(f) permits secondary aid in other administrative and civil court proceedings only exceptionally, where the need follows from concrete life circumstances; Articles 14 and 19 add means and scope rules; and Article 12(2) includes possible court-cost and court-fee relief. The statutory text does not resolve whether a particular EU-family-member visa appeal or residence proceeding qualifies. Keep that question open until the eligibility provisions, special immigration rules, procedure type and actual remedy are crosswalked.

NN 142/25 is accepted only as the year-specific 2025 compensation regulation. Article 7 prices provider work in administrative disputes; it does not determine the applicant's eligibility, provide a fee exemption, or establish the 2026 provider rate. The latter remains unresolved.

## Residence-card specimen and transition review

NN 86/21 is not only an amendment text: Article 11 makes the new Form 4b and Form 11b images integral annexes. Article 27a identifies Form 11b as the card issued to a non-EEA family member. Form 2b is the application form, not the card specimen. The four official specimen images were visually reviewed at native resolution on 25 October 2026 and retained under the accepted parent-law document. Regulation (EU) 2025/1208 Article 6 concerns residence documents issued to Union citizens; Article 7 sets format and code requirements for non-EU family-member cards. Its official Croatian text prescribes the same Article 10/20 code wording as NN 86/21 Article 27a(5), so the wording comparison is resolved. The blank Form 11b image does not prove the code appears in field [10] on current MUP cards or establish their technical features. Article 8’s rule for existing non-EU family-member cards has distinct classes: cards missing minimum ICAO security or a functional MRZ are not valid; other Article 7-nonconforming cards generally cease at expiry or 3 August 2026, except those meeting the specified ICAO and Annex points (c) and (g) conditions, which may remain until expiry or 3 August 2031. These conditions cannot be assessed from the blank template. No conclusion is made about current cards issued by MUP; that evidence check remains urgent and open.

The live MUP forms index is recorded separately from MUP’s legal index. Its EEA heading cites NN 107/20, 148/20 and 86/21, while the current MUP legislation index also lists NN 155/22. The difference remains documented; the live MUP form links are the current download locators, while MUP’s Propisi index supplies the listed legal-version chain. The English gov.hr service page is also recorded as a procedural lead, not as an accepted source.

## Fee and effective-remedy dependency review (19 October 2026)

- The current Administrative Fees Tariff item 30 (NN 130/25) sets €130 for an appeal against refusal, annulment or revocation of a visa and specified visa-based-stay decisions. The current MVEP page states €130 or USD 150.71, a 15-day appeal period, a 60-day decision period, Croatian-language filing with a narrow English exception, and a 30-day court route. The older MVEP page is excluded as a stale comparator; it gives an obsolete amount and older law chain.
- Directive 2004/38 Article 5(2) makes the necessary entry visa free for qualifying family members. Commission guidance describes this as the visa-issuance fee and says the Directive does not set other visa-issuance procedures. This does not answer whether the separate Croatian appeal fee applies to those family members or whether an exemption or waiver is available. Preserve that as an unresolved legal question; do not declare the fee either compatible or incompatible from the tariff alone.
- Court Fees Tariff NN 50/26 is effective 1 June 2026. It sets €110 for an administrative claim and €140 for appeals and specified High Administrative Court filings; NN 37/23 remains applicable to administrative disputes commenced before the effective date. Court Fees Act Article 22(1) says administrative-dispute fees are payable only if the court rejects the claim or dismisses the action. Review outcome-based cost exposure, appeal-stage fees, legal aid and effective access separately from an up-front filing fee.
- Eight other fee and appeal records remain located but uncaptured. The current MVEP visa-appeal page is now captured but unaccepted; the NN 142/25 legal-aid provider tariff is captured and accepted for 2025 only. Current fee rules still need source acceptance, and legal-aid provider remuneration for 2026 remains unresolved. Corresponding topic layers remain incomplete and no legal topic is marked complete.

- Additional dependency lead: the Ministry of Justice lists NN 142/25 as the 2025 annual regulation setting provider remuneration for secondary legal aid, including administrative disputes. This affects aid-delivery capacity, but it does not establish applicant eligibility or court-fee exemption. A 2026 legal-aid Act amendment appears in the ministry consultation plan only and is not enacted law.


The four new SIS/VIS PDFs have accepted local copies and separately reviewed link records. The current MUP and MVEP pages support only the displayed routes; MUP’s SIS page has a citation typo and unverified contact details, and the MVEP form does not include the restriction request mentioned on its guidance page. Visual layout review of these new forms and full privacy/oversight issue coverage remain open.

## Current fee and remedy issue

The current Administrative Fees Tariff sets €130 for the specified visa appeal; MVEP states €130 or USD 150.71. Whether that fee applies to qualifying Directive 2004/38 family members, or is subject to a waiver, remains unresolved. Court Fees Tariff NN 50/26 sets €110 for an administrative claim and €140 for appeals/selected High Administrative Court filings, but Court Fees Act Article 22(1) makes administrative-dispute fees payable only after the claim is rejected or the action is dismissed. The court amounts are not described here as mandatory up-front filing fees. The 2026 annual secondary-legal-aid provider compensation status remains unresolved: NN 142/25 is accepted for 2025 only, and the Ministry index and targeted search do not prove no 2026 instrument exists. The Free Legal Aid Act, its amendment, the 2026 budgetary-base source, and NN 142/25 are accepted as official text. The Ministry index and overview remain unaccepted; the MPUDT conditions page still displays legacy kuna amounts and is excluded from current-value use.

## Findings requiring resolution

1. MUP’s current Propisi index identifies the latest versions/updates it lists for the EEA Act and regulation, Foreigners Act through NN 55/26, and third-country residence/procedure rules through NN 43/26 and NN 116/26. A separate older index is stale. Prefer a government-published consolidated text where available; otherwise use the closest government-published version and state its cutoff. Do not assemble all listed fragments just to create a synthetic consolidation; consult a specific amendment only for a material issue.
2. MVEP’s visa regulations index and refusal guidance still cite 2020/2022 Foreigners Act versions. Use the closest government-published current legal text and check the named 2025/2026 amendments only for provisions that affect the refusal process or deadlines.
3. The AZOP candidate previously labelled as a personal-data statute is an index. The actual GDPR implementation Act NN 42/18 is now separately listed.
4. NN 85/15 and 69/22 are located as specific change records for the Access to Information Act. Use the latest government-published consolidated text where available; do not reconstruct every amendment if it is not.
5. All 42 Directive 2004/38 articles have explicit mapping rows (including a reasoned historical disposition for Article 42), but operative article-to-national-source concordance remains unvalidated. The post-transition residence-card specimens, administrative remedies, court coverage, missions, and local police practice remain open.
6. Copyright Act NN 111/21 Article 18(3) applies narrowly to the individually identified official legal texts and the one unchanged MVEP VIS form; MUP Terms of Use expressly permit the seven retained MUP forms with source attribution. Twenty-five selector-bound text fragments and 51 raw responses are in the Monitor local-only archive, not in the country pack. Publisher shells, general website guidance, third-party material and database-wide reuse remain outside the accepted scope.
7. The official documents include 160 embedded image assets across six records, including 83 images in the current NN 116/26 third-country procedure amendment. They are inventoried but not visually classified or checked against live forms.

No country-complete conclusion is supported. Empty folders or placeholders are not used as evidence of coverage.

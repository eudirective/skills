## Identity and scope

This is the regulatory Book II of CESEDA, “Dispositions applicables aux citoyens de l'Union européenne et aux membres de leur famille,” Articles R. 210-1 to R. 286-2. The section is part of the official consolidated code on Légifrance, maintained by DILA. The text page opened with the version in force at 9 September 2026.

The regulatory part supplies implementing rules and procedures for the parallel legislative Book II (Articles L. 200-1 to L. 286-2). Many provisions expressly depend on those legislative articles. Read the two parts together. This file covers only the regulatory section and does not claim to reproduce or independently establish the current legislative provisions.

## Substantive areas confirmed on the live section

### Entry and visa facilitation

Articles R. 221-1 and R. 221-2 address admission documents. R. 221-1 states the valid identity-card or passport route for EU citizens. R. 221-2 addresses admission of third-country family members, the residence-card-based visa exemption, and free, accelerated consular visa issuance when the family link is established. Its scope expressly extends to the persons referenced in Articles L. 200-4 and L. 200-5. Apply the linked legislative definitions and any relevant EU-law interpretation; this section alone is not a complete eligibility test.

### Residence, retention and documents

Title III covers registration attestations, conditions for stays beyond three months, retention of residence rights, residence documents and permanent residence. The page includes rules on the evidence of identity, health insurance and sufficient resources for relevant residence categories; provisions for workers, inactive persons and students; retention in specified employment-interruption or family-separation circumstances; and residence-document forms, validity and supporting evidence.

The section distinguishes regulatory procedures from the underlying statutory conditions. In particular, residence rights and family eligibility depend on Book II of the legislative part. The specific text and effective date of each article should be checked on its own Légifrance page when applying a rule.

### Removal, execution, controls and litigation

Title V contains rules for removal decisions, including the competent administrative authority, the period stated in notifications, and a cross-reference for administrative and judicial procedure. Title VI addresses execution of removal decisions; it cross-refers to regulatory provisions on enforcement and detention. Title VII sets sanctions for certain registration or residence-card failures. Title VII bis makes Book IX rules applicable to litigation for people whose situation is governed by Book II. These cross-references require reading the relevant current provisions in the linked codes. At least one territorial adaptation, Article R. 281-5 for Mayotte, showed an article-level effective date of 13 June 2026 and a reference to Decree 2026-471 of 10 June 2026. The territorial adaptations therefore need article-level review; do not assume metropolitan provisions apply identically everywhere.

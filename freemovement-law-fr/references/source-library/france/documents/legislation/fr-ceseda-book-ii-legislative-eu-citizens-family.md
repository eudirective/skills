## Identity and scope

This is legislative Book II of CESEDA, “Dispositions applicables aux citoyens de l'Union européenne et aux membres de leur famille,” Articles L. 200-1 to L. 286-2. It sets rules for entry, residence and removal for EU citizens, people assimilated to EU citizens, their family members, and certain other people with private and family ties to EU citizens.

The official Légifrance page displayed “Version en vigueur au 24/08/2026” when reviewed on 29 October 2026. The page is unpinned and follows the maintained section. Article-level currentness can differ from the section header, especially in the overseas provisions.

## Main legislative rules identified

### Persons covered and family categories

- **Articles L. 200-1 and L. 200-2** define the purpose of Book II and EU citizens.
- **Article L. 200-3** assimilates Icelandic, Liechtenstein, Norwegian and Swiss nationals to EU citizens for this Book's purposes, subject to the Book's conditions.
- **Article L. 200-4** defines family members by specified relationships, including spouses, certain direct descendants under 21 or dependent, and dependent direct ascendants.
- **Article L. 200-5** separately covers certain other private and family ties, including dependency or household membership in the country of departure, serious health grounds requiring personal care, and durable non-marital private and family ties. The provision calls for examination of the person's individual circumstances.
- **Article L. 200-6** limits restrictions on free movement and residence to conduct constituting a real, current and sufficiently serious threat to a fundamental interest of society, from a public-order and public-security perspective.

The statutory categories in L. 200-4 and L. 200-5 are not interchangeable. This summary does not extend them beyond their wording or the relevant EU-law interpretation.

### Entry and short stays

Title II requires entry documents as specified by decree and provides that, before refusal of entry where documents are missing, the person must be given reasonable means to obtain them or otherwise establish beneficiary status.

**Article L. 232-1** provides for stays of up to three months for EU citizens and covered family members, subject to the public-assistance limitation and ordinary entry conditions. The linked regulatory rules and EU law govern application.

### Residence beyond three months and residence documents

Title III distinguishes the EU citizen's residence right from possession of a residence document. **Article L. 231-1** says EU citizens are not required to hold a residence document, though one is issued on request. **Article L. 231-2** provides for registration with the mayor for EU citizens intending to establish habitual residence in France, within three months after arrival.

**Article L. 233-1** sets the main bases for residence beyond three months: professional activity; sufficient resources and health insurance; qualifying study with insurance and resources; or accompanying/joining qualifying citizens under the listed conditions. **Article L. 233-2** addresses third-country family members of qualifying EU citizens. Related provisions address other family/private-tie cases, transitional labor-market measures, residence-card requirements and the circumstances for permanent residence.

**Articles L. 234-1 to L. 234-3** govern permanent residence. The page states the general five-year lawful and uninterrupted residence route for EU citizens and certain family members, along with loss after an absence of more than two consecutive years and special cases.

This is a statutory outline only. It does not replace the relevant regulatory provisions, proof requirements, or the conditions in linked EU instruments.

### Important Conseil d'État annotation on Articles L. 233-1 and L. 233-2

The official Légifrance page displays the Conseil d'État decision nos. 450285 and 450288 of 24 February 2022 as an annotation to L. 233-1 and L. 233-2. The decision partially annulled those provisions insofar as they excluded residence beyond three months for a dependent child of an EU citizen who came to study or train in France but was not that citizen's direct descendant. The current text must be read with that decision and the linked Conseil d'État follow-up after CJEU judgment C-143/22. Do not rely on the bare wording of the amended-code display without checking those decisions.

### Removal, safeguards and procedure

Titles V and VI cover removal, execution and related measures; Title VII addresses controls and sanctions; Title VII bis makes Book IX litigation provisions applicable. The section includes public-order grounds tied to a real, current and sufficiently serious threat, individualized consideration, notice of voluntary-departure periods, restrictions on movement, and court-challenge routes. Specific deadlines, competent courts, enforceability and procedural steps depend on the exact measure and the linked CESEDA/CJA provisions.

### Overseas provisions

Title VIII adapts the Code for overseas territories and Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon. The text contains territory-specific tables and exceptions. For example, Articles L. 283-1 and L. 284-1 displayed changes effective 18 July 2025 under Ordinance 2025-646. Confirm the correct territorial branch rather than applying the metropolitan version by default.

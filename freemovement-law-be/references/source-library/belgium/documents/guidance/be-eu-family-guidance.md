## Scope and page condition

The overview identifies a route for family members of EU citizens and citizens of associated countries (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland). It is JavaScript-dependent and its rendered text is incomplete. The linked detailed guidance is substantially readable but has malformed terms and cross-references in its rendering. The summary below records only the route distinctions that can be read consistently; it does not reproduce either page.

The detailed page identifies Articles 40bis and 47/2 of the Law of 15 December 1980, as amended by the Law of 10 March 2024 effective 1 September 2024. The official consolidated law and applicable EU law remain the controlling legal sources.

## Route distinctions stated by the guidance

The detailed page describes family members and “other family members” who may accompany or join an EU or associated-country citizen in Belgium, subject to conditions. It identifies spouses and marriage-equivalent registered partners, other legally registered partners, qualifying direct descendants, and specified ascendants. For other family members it names durable partners, dependent or household members, and persons whose serious health condition requires personal care by the EU citizen. The statutory categories and conditions are not interchangeable; consult the current law and the beneficiary-specific official page.

For a stay exceeding 90 days, the page directs applicants to apply for family reunification with the municipal administration for the place of residence in Belgium. For a short stay, a visa-required family member may need a C visa. The page describes Directive 2004/38/EC treatment only where the EU citizen's movement is covered by the Directive and the family member accompanies or joins that citizen. It gives examples including movement to another Member State and certain return situations. The ordinary national Visa Code route may apply where the movement is not covered.

The guidance describes supporting evidence categories such as identity, family relationship, the citizen's qualifying movement or residence, and the applicant's accompanying/joining link. Other-family-member cases require evidence relevant to the specific category. It also states special visa-processing arrangements for Directive cases, subject to consular representation arrangements.

## Legal and maintenance limits

- The overview has no displayed version date and its text is incomplete; the detailed linked page has malformed rendering. Recheck both official pages and the current consolidated Law of 15 December 1980 before relying on particulars.
- The linked page for family members who are themselves EU citizens could not be retrieved during this review. This note therefore does not summarize that separate procedure.
- The page's references to EU/associated-country citizens do not establish that every route or entitlement is identical for EU, EEA, Swiss, and Belgian sponsors.
- This note is an independently worded locator and limited source summary, not a transcription, a complete account of the law, or an assessment of an individual case.

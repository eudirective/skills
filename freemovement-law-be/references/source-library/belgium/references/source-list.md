# Belgium source discovery register

Research date: 21 October 2026
Scope: Belgian national, federal, regional, mission, court, and oversight sources related to EU/EEA/Swiss free movement and third-country family members, entry and residence, administration, remedies, privacy, and access to records. The comparison leads were the Denmark, Sweden, Czechia, Hungary, and Romania source reviews. Belgium is an EU Member State; its national routes are checked independently.

This is a bounded discovery register and partial local-text pack, not a complete inventory of Belgian law. The 39 earlier records were reviewed as candidate links and local conversion artifacts. Nine official-link candidates have since been added. External links are accepted separately from local copies and topic coverage: 47 official links are accepted within their recorded scope, one superseded IBZ page is not relied upon, and thirty local copies are accepted: nine Justel legal texts, fourteen official guidance/procedure/oversight snapshots, three IBZ forms/specimens, the FPS Foreign Affairs visa-routing matrix, two regional public-information laws and one selected-case publication. No topic is marked covered. Belgium review remains incomplete. The machine-readable records are `candidate-manifest.json` and `source-acceptance.json`.

## Search and coverage notes

- The accepted list combines federal Justel and IBZ sources, FPS Foreign Affairs and mission routes, oversight bodies, Wallonia/Wallex, the Flemish Codex, Brussels and COCOF services, Gallilex, and the German-speaking Community legal database. N-Lex is only an EU discovery route.
- The official German-speaking Community legal database states that it offers coordinated current DG decrees and orders, selected federal texts in German and related parliamentary documents. It also states that only the original Gazette text is legally binding. Its full service is listed for human review because the exact 1995 and 2022 decree records have not yet been located.
- The Data Protection Act of 30 July 2018 is now included through French and Dutch official Justel locators, supported by federal government references. Review the current consolidated record directly on Justel.
- Only UK and UAE mission pages were reviewed. The national competence matrix is a routing aid, not a review of every applicant-residence country, local checklist or outsourced provider.
- Federal, regional, community, provincial and municipal access-to-records routes have different scopes. The 1994 federal law is not a nationwide answer.
- The RvV/CCE selected monthly publication is accepted as a discovery route only; the publication is selective and does not establish full judgment or case-law coverage.
- Annex 19ter is accepted as the live index-linked specimen only. Confirm the current revision and legal/card classification before operational use.
- The earlier public-mirror inventory included five candidate records. The 1980 Immigration Law and 1981 Royal Decree were reconciled with corrected Justel identifiers; former IDs remain as aliases. The old IBZ family page is superseded.
- The record set is bounded and no complete native-language search transcript or all-mission inventory was retained. This limits completeness claims but does not block ordinary use of the accepted official links.

## Current review state

The federal 1994 access-to-records law is not a nationwide answer. Wallonia, Flanders, Brussels institutions, the German-speaking Community, the French Community, and municipal/provincial authorities need scope-specific checks. The relevant official services are listed in `source-acceptance.json` for human review where current consolidated wording or institutional competence needs confirmation.

No exhaustive query transcript or all-mission inventory exists. The source register does not establish complete law coverage, a complete remedies map, or a complete court corpus. It does establish that official links can be used without requiring a retained capture or custom automated pipeline.
